## OKR Architecture

### Objective 1 — Build a profitable, financially disciplined growth engine
- KR1: Establish verified revenue, COGS, gross margin, operating expense, cash, and profitability baselines.
- KR2: Establish SKU/channel-level unit economics.
- KR3: Set approved profitability targets only after baseline validation.

### Objective 2 — Build a reliable product-to-customer operating system
- KR1: Establish measurable product, sourcing, manufacturing, QA, inventory, logistics, and fulfillment KPIs.
- KR2: Establish weekly operational exception reporting.
- KR3: Define accountable owners and escalation thresholds for material risks.

### Objective 3 — Build a measurable customer and revenue engine
- KR1: Establish verified sales, marketing, e-commerce, conversion, retention, and service baselines.
- KR2: Establish channel-level acquisition and profitability measurement.
- KR3: Set growth targets only after baseline and capacity constraints are validated.

### Objective 4 — Build scalable executive governance
- KR1: Maintain one authoritative KPI register.
- KR2: Conduct recurring CEO operating reviews using actual-versus-target performance.
- KR3: Track strategic initiatives, risks, decisions, owners, deadlines, and outcomes.

## Target-Setting Rule
Numerical targets must be based on verified baseline performance, capacity, cash constraints, market evidence, product economics, and strategic ambition. No arbitrary or fabricated targets become official OKRs.

## Operating Cadence
- **Weekly:** KPI exceptions, cash, sales, production, inventory, delivery, customer issues, critical risks.
- **Monthly:** Financial/operational performance and initiative review.
- **Quarterly:** OKR scoring, strategic review, resource allocation, target adjustment.
- **Annually:** Strategy, budget, organization, product portfolio, and expansion plan.
