**Status:** Active — CEO HQ Controlled Execution Register  
**Authority:** CEO Headquarters  
**Version:** v1.0  
**Review Cadence:** Weekly / Monthly / Quarterly / Annual

---

## 1. Purpose

The Executive Action Register is the controlled execution layer connecting:

**Company Strategy → Company OKRs → Department Execution → KPI Measurement → Risk Management → CEO Review**

Every material company initiative must have:

- Accountable owner
- Priority
- Deliverable
- KPI
- Target date
- Dependencies
- Risk assessment
- CEO decision status

No material initiative should proceed to production execution without appropriate ownership, measurement, risk review, and executive authorization.

---

## 2. Governance Rules

All material actions must:

1. Align with approved company strategy and OKRs.
2. Have one accountable owner.
3. Have measurable success criteria.
4. Identify dependencies.
5. Identify material risks.
6. Have an approved priority.
7. Have a target date or defined ongoing cadence.
8. Follow applicable departmental governance.
9. Follow the repository's protected-branch and pull-request requirements.
10. Be recorded in this register.

No department may optimize its own KPI at the expense of company-wide objectives.

## 5. Mandatory Action Fields

Every new P0, P1, P2 or P3 action must contain:

- Action ID
- Strategic objective
- Department owner
- Priority
- Deliverable
- KPI
- Baseline
- Target
- Target date
- Dependencies
- Risk
- Risk owner
- Mitigation
- Budget requirement
- CEO decision
- Status
- Review date

Where reliable data does not yet exist, the field must be explicitly marked **TBD** rather than estimated.

## 6. P0 and P1 Risk Requirements

Every P0 and P1 action must identify:

- Probability
- Impact
- Mitigation
- Contingency
- Monitoring KPI
- Escalation trigger

Material risks must be escalated to CEO Headquarters.

## 9. Financial Control

Any action requiring material expenditure must identify:

- Estimated investment
- Operating cost
- Expected financial benefit
- Cash-flow impact
- Gross-margin impact
- ROI or strategic justification
- Finance & Accounting review

Major spending requires explicit executive approval.

---

## 10. Change Control

Changes to this register are controlled company changes.

Any material modification must:

1. Be made on an approved working branch.
2. Be submitted through a pull request.
3. Pass required automated checks.
4. Receive required review.
5. Be merged through the protected repository workflow.
6. Be reflected in the executive decision record where material.

Direct unauthorized modification of protected production branches is prohibited.

## 12. Status Definitions

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proposed | Action identified but not yet approved |
| Approved | CEO-approved for execution |
| Active | Execution underway |
| Blocked | Execution prevented by unresolved dependency or risk |
| At Risk | Execution may miss approved outcome |
| Complete | Deliverable completed and verified |
| Cancelled | Executive decision to stop the action |
| On Hold | Temporarily paused by executive decision |

---

## 13. Executive Accountability

CEO Headquarters is responsible for:

- Strategic alignment
- Priority decisions
- Resource allocation
- Cross-department conflict resolution
- Risk escalation
- Executive approvals
- KPI review
- Action-register governance

Department heads are accountable for execution of actions assigned to their departments.

---

## 14. Approval Standard

An action may be marked **Approved** only when:

- Owner is identified.
- Objective is strategically aligned.
- Deliverable is defined.
- KPI is measurable.
- Dependencies are understood.
- Material risks are assessed.
- Required budget review is completed.
- Required executive authorization is obtained.

## 16. Success Criteria

The Executive Action Register will be considered operationally successful when:

1. 100% of material company initiatives have accountable owners.
2. 100% of P0/P1 actions have documented risk controls.
3. 100% of active material actions have measurable KPIs.
4. Executive decisions are recorded and traceable.
5. Department execution can be reviewed against company strategy.
6. Weekly and monthly review cadences are operating.
7. Material changes follow the controlled GitHub workflow.
