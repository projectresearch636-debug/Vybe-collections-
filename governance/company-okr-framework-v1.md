## 1. Purpose

The Company OKR Framework establishes the strategic execution system for Vybe Collections.

The framework connects:

**Company Vision → Strategic Priorities → Company Objectives → Key Results → Department Ownership → KPIs → Initiatives → Execution → Measurement → Corrective Action**

The framework is designed to:

- Translate company strategy into measurable objectives.
- Align all departments around common priorities.
- Establish measurable company-level outcomes.
- Connect departmental KPIs with strategic objectives.
- Identify cross-department dependencies.
- Improve accountability.
- Support resource allocation.
- Provide a structured executive review system.
- Enable corrective action when objectives are at risk.

---

# 2. OKR Principles

Vybe Collections shall follow these principles:

1. Objectives define what the company intends to achieve.
2. Key Results define measurable outcomes.
3. Initiatives define the work required to influence Key Results.
4. KPIs monitor ongoing business performance.
5. Each Objective must have an accountable owner.
6. Each Key Result must have a measurable definition.
7. Targets must be based on evidence whenever possible.
8. No performance result may be fabricated.
9. Cross-department dependencies must be documented.
10. OKRs must support company strategy rather than replace operational KPIs.

---

# 3. OKR Hierarchy

The company OKR hierarchy is:

### Level 1 — Company Vision

Defines the long-term direction of Vybe Collections.

### Level 2 — Strategic Priorities

Defines the major areas requiring company-wide focus.

### Level 3 — Company Objectives

Defines what must be achieved during the OKR cycle.

### Level 4 — Key Results

Defines measurable outcomes that determine whether an Objective is being achieved.

### Level 5 — Department Objectives

Translate company objectives into departmental outcomes.

### Level 6 — Initiatives

Define the projects and actions required to influence Key Results.

### Level 7 — KPIs

Measure ongoing operational performance.

---

# 4. OKR Cycle

The standard OKR cycle shall follow:

**Set → Align → Approve → Execute → Monitor → Review → Score → Learn → Reset**

## Phase 1 — Set

CEO Headquarters establishes strategic priorities and proposed company objectives.

## Phase 2 — Align

Departments identify their contribution to company objectives.

## Phase 3 — Approve

CEO Headquarters approves the final OKRs.

## Phase 4 — Execute

Departments execute approved initiatives.

## Phase 5 — Monitor

Performance is monitored through KPIs and Key Results.

## Phase 6 — Review

Weekly and monthly reviews identify risks and progress.

## Phase 7 — Score

At the end of the OKR cycle, Key Results are scored using verified data.

## Phase 8 — Learn

The company evaluates:

- What worked
- What failed
- Why performance differed
- What should continue
- What should stop
- What should change

## Phase 9 — Reset

The next OKR cycle incorporates verified lessons and strategic changes.

# 19. OKR and KPI Relationship

OKRs and KPIs serve different purposes.

### OKRs

Measure strategic change and outcomes.

### KPIs

Measure ongoing business health and operational performance.

Example:

**Objective:** Improve Digital Commerce

**Key Result:** Increase website conversion rate

**KPI:** Website conversion rate

**Initiatives:**

- Improve product pages
- Improve checkout experience
- Improve website performance
- Improve campaign landing pages

A KPI may support multiple Objectives but must maintain one primary accountable owner.

# 21. OKR Scoring

Unless otherwise approved, Key Results shall use a 0.0–1.0 scoring scale.

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 0.0 | No measurable progress |
| 0.1–0.3 | Limited progress |
| 0.4–0.6 | Moderate progress |
| 0.7–0.9 | Strong progress |
| 1.0 | Target achieved |
| >1.0 | Target exceeded where measurement methodology permits |

Scores must be based on verified results.

The scoring system should not encourage manipulation of targets or reporting.

---

# 22. Objective Health

Objective health shall be classified as:

| Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| 🟢 On Track | Key Results progressing toward target |
| 🟡 At Risk | One or more Key Results may miss target |
| 🔴 Critical | Objective is materially unlikely to be achieved |
| ⚪ Not Started | Execution has not started |
| 🔵 Completed | Objective cycle completed |
| ⚫ Escalated | CEO Headquarters decision required |

# 27. Corrective Action

When an Objective becomes 🔴 Critical or materially 🟡 At Risk:

1. Identify the variance.
2. Identify the root cause.
3. Identify responsible dependencies.
4. Assess business impact.
5. Define corrective action.
6. Assign accountable owner.
7. Set deadline.
8. Allocate approved resources.
9. Monitor progress.
10. Escalate unresolved material issues to CEO Headquarters.

# 28. OKR Governance

CEO Headquarters is responsible for:

- Approving company Objectives
- Approving strategic priorities
- Resolving cross-department conflicts
- Approving material resource allocation
- Reviewing company performance
- Approving strategic changes
- Reviewing final OKR scores

Department Heads are responsible for:

- Department OKRs
- Execution
- KPI reporting
- Dependency management
- Risk identification
- Corrective actions

Data Analytics & BI is responsible for:

- Data validation
- Reporting
- KPI/OKR measurement support
- Dashboard integrity

---

# 29. OKR Change Control

Objectives and Key Results should not be changed casually during an active cycle.

Changes may be approved when:

- Company strategy materially changes.
- External conditions materially change.
- A major business assumption becomes invalid.
- Regulatory requirements change.
- A major operational disruption occurs.
- Executive priorities are formally changed.

All material changes must be documented with:

- Original Objective/KR
- Reason for change
- Impact
- New Objective/KR
- Approval
- Date
- Owner
