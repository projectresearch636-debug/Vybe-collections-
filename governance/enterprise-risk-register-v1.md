# Vybe Collections — Enterprise Risk Register v1

**Status:** Active — CEO HQ Controlled Risk Register  
**Authority:** CEO Headquarters  
**Version:** v1.0  
**Review Cadence:** Weekly / Monthly / Quarterly / Annual

---

## 1. Purpose

The Enterprise Risk Register establishes the company-wide framework for identifying, assessing, controlling, monitoring, escalating, and reviewing material business risks.

It connects:

**Company Strategy → Department Execution → Risk Identification → Risk Controls → KPI Monitoring → CEO Escalation → Executive Decision**

The purpose of this register is to ensure that material risks are identified before they become material business failures.

---

## 2. Risk Management Principles

Vybe Collections will manage risk according to the following principles:

1. Risks must be identified proactively.
2. Every material risk must have an accountable owner.
3. Risk assessments must use defined criteria.
4. P0 and P1 risks require documented mitigation.
5. Material risks must have monitoring KPIs.
6. Escalation triggers must be defined.
7. Risk controls must be proportionate to potential impact.
8. Risk decisions must consider company-wide consequences.
9. Risk acceptance requires appropriate executive authority.
10. Risk records must remain version-controlled.

No department may accept material risk solely to optimize its departmental KPI.

## 4. Risk Scoring Method

Each material risk must receive:

- Probability score
- Impact score
- Overall risk score

### Probability

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Rare |
| 2 | Unlikely |
| 3 | Possible |
| 4 | Likely |
| 5 | Almost Certain |

### Impact

| Score | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Minimal |
| 2 | Minor |
| 3 | Moderate |
| 4 | Major |
| 5 | Critical |

### Risk Score

**Risk Score = Probability × Impact**

| Score | Risk Level | Required Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low | Monitor |
| 5–9 | Moderate | Department mitigation |
| 10–14 | High | Department + CEO HQ review |
| 15–19 | Very High | Immediate executive mitigation |
| 20–25 | Critical | P0 escalation and executive action |

---

## 5. Priority Classification

| Priority | Definition |
|---|---|
| P0 | Critical company-level risk requiring immediate executive control |
| P1 | High-priority strategic or operational risk |
| P2 | Important operational risk requiring active monitoring |
| P3 | Lower-level risk or optimization concern |

P0 and P1 risks require documented mitigation, ownership, monitoring, and escalation criteria.

## 7. Mandatory Risk Fields

Every material risk must contain:

- Risk ID
- Risk category
- Risk description
- Risk owner
- Priority
- Probability
- Impact
- Risk score
- Mitigation
- Contingency
- Monitoring KPI
- Escalation trigger
- Current status
- Review date
- CEO decision where applicable

If reliable information is unavailable, the field must be marked **TBD** rather than estimated.

---

## 8. Risk Ownership

Each risk must have one accountable risk owner.

Supporting departments may contribute mitigation activities, but accountability cannot be distributed without a clearly identified primary owner.

The CEO Headquarters retains executive oversight of all P0 risks and material enterprise risks.

---

## 9. P0 Risk Requirements

Every P0 risk must have:

1. Named executive owner.
2. Documented mitigation plan.
3. Documented contingency plan.
4. Monitoring KPI.
5. Escalation trigger.
6. Review cadence.
7. Executive decision status.

P0 risks must be reviewed at every applicable CEO Headquarters operating review.

---

## 10. P1 Risk Requirements

Every P1 risk must have:

- Named owner
- Probability assessment
- Impact assessment
- Risk score
- Mitigation
- Contingency
- Monitoring KPI
- Escalation trigger
- Defined review cadence

Unmanaged P1 risks must be escalated to CEO Headquarters.

## 12. Risk Monitoring

Risk monitoring must use measurable indicators wherever practical.

Examples include:

- Cash position
- Gross margin
- Revenue variance
- Inventory turnover
- Supplier delivery performance
- Manufacturing efficiency
- Product defect rate
- Return rate
- Customer satisfaction
- Customer retention
- Website conversion
- Marketing ROI
- Sales growth
- Employee productivity
- System availability
- Security incidents
- Compliance incidents

Where no reliable data exists, the responsible department must first establish the measurement method.

---

## 13. Escalation Rules

A risk must be escalated to CEO Headquarters when:

- It becomes P0.
- Its score reaches the critical range.
- A mitigation plan fails.
- A material escalation trigger is breached.
- It threatens company strategy.
- It creates material financial exposure.
- It creates material legal or compliance exposure.
- It threatens customer or brand trust.
- It materially affects multiple departments.
- Executive resources or capital are required.

---

## 14. Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance is an explicit executive decision.

A material risk may not be considered accepted merely because no mitigation activity has been initiated.

Risk acceptance must document:

- Risk ID
- Reason for acceptance
- Financial/operational rationale
- Responsible executive
- Review date
- Conditions or limits
- Escalation requirements

---

## 15. Financial Risk Controls

Material financial risks must be evaluated for:

- Cash-flow impact
- Revenue impact
- Gross-margin impact
- Operating-cost impact
- Capital requirement
- Potential loss
- Recovery cost
- ROI implications

Finance & Accounting must participate in material decisions involving significant financial exposure.

---

## 16. Operational Risk Controls

Operational risks must be assessed for:

- Capacity
- Quality
- Delivery
- Supplier dependency
- Inventory
- Workforce
- Technology dependency
- Customer impact
- Business continuity

Material operational disruptions require documented recovery or contingency planning.

---

## 17. Business Continuity

For critical business processes, responsible departments should identify:

- Critical process
- Primary dependency
- Failure scenario
- Maximum tolerable disruption
- Manual fallback
- Recovery procedure
- Responsible owner
- Escalation contact

Business continuity requirements should be prioritized according to business impact.
