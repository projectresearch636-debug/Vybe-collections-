# Supply Chain & Logistics Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for Supply Chain & Logistics at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to ensure that materials, inventory, finished products, customer orders, and returns move through the supply chain accurately, efficiently, reliably, and cost-effectively.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

- Maintain accurate inventory visibility.
- Coordinate material and finished-goods movement.
- Establish controlled receiving and warehousing processes.
- Improve order fulfillment accuracy and speed.
- Support reliable dispatch and delivery.
- Control logistics costs.
- Monitor carrier and logistics-partner performance.
- Establish controlled returns and reverse logistics.
- Reduce stockouts, excess inventory, losses, and delivery failures.
- Maintain supply-chain traceability.
- Establish measurable supply-chain and logistics KPIs.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**Procurement/Production Readiness → Inbound Logistics → Receiving → Inventory → Warehousing → Order Allocation → Picking → Packing → Dispatch → Delivery → Returns → Reverse Logistics → Reconciliation → Performance Review**

It applies to raw materials, production inputs, finished goods, customer orders, and returned products where applicable.

# 5. Supply Chain Readiness

Before material or finished-goods movement is initiated, required information should be available.

Readiness may include:

- Product/material identification
- Quantity
- Source/destination
- Required delivery date
- Packaging requirements
- Quality status
- Documentation
- Shipping requirements
- Responsible owner
- Required approvals

Incomplete information should be treated as a supply-chain risk.

---

# 6. Inbound Logistics

Inbound shipments should be coordinated against expected deliveries.

Inbound records should identify, where applicable:

- Supplier
- Purchase/order reference
- Material/product
- Quantity
- Shipment date
- Expected arrival date
- Carrier
- Tracking information
- Receiving location
- Quality status

Unexpected or delayed inbound shipments should be documented and escalated according to impact.

---

# 7. Receiving

Received materials and products should be verified against available documentation.

Receiving controls may include:

- Quantity verification
- Product/material identification
- Packaging condition
- Visible damage inspection
- Documentation verification
- Quality status
- Lot/batch identification where applicable

Receiving discrepancies should be documented and resolved.

---

# 8. Inventory Management

Inventory records should provide accurate visibility of available stock.

Inventory categories may include:

- Raw materials
- Work in progress
- Finished goods
- Packaging
- Reserved inventory
- Damaged inventory
- Returned inventory
- Quarantined inventory
- Rejected inventory

Inventory status must be clearly identified to prevent unauthorized use or sale.

---

# 9. Inventory Accuracy

Inventory accuracy should be maintained through:

- Controlled stock movements
- Receiving records
- Dispatch records
- Return records
- Cycle counts
- Periodic physical inventory reconciliation
- Investigation of variances

Inventory discrepancies should be documented and investigated according to materiality and risk.

---

# 10. Inventory Controls

Inventory movement should be recorded for:

- Receipt
- Transfer
- Allocation
- Production consumption
- Adjustment
- Dispatch
- Return
- Disposal

Unauthorized inventory adjustments should not be permitted.

Material adjustments require appropriate authorization and documentation.

---

# 11. Inventory Planning

Inventory planning should consider:

- Demand forecasts
- Production schedules
- Supplier lead times
- Manufacturing lead times
- Customer orders
- Safety-stock requirements
- Seasonal demand
- Minimum order quantities
- Inventory holding costs
- Product lifecycle

Inventory planning assumptions must be documented and reviewed as new data becomes available.

---

# 12. Inventory Aging

Inventory should be monitored for aging.

Potential categories include:

- Current
- Slow-moving
- Aging
- Excess
- Obsolete

Aging thresholds should be defined according to product category and company operating conditions.

Aging inventory should be escalated for appropriate commercial or operational action.

---

# 13. Warehousing

Warehouse operations should maintain controlled processes for:

- Receiving
- Inspection
- Put-away
- Storage
- Inventory identification
- Picking
- Packing
- Dispatch
- Returns
- Stock reconciliation

Warehouse layouts and processes should support accuracy, safety, efficiency, and product protection.

---

# 14. Product Storage

Products should be stored in a manner that protects against:

- Damage
- Contamination
- Moisture
- Unauthorized access
- Misidentification
- Loss
- Excessive handling

Storage conditions should be appropriate to the product and material characteristics.

---

# 15. Order Fulfillment

Order fulfillment should follow:

**Order Received → Order Validated → Inventory Allocated → Picked → Checked → Packed → Dispatched → Delivered → Closed**

Order exceptions should be identified and escalated promptly.

---

# 16. Order Allocation

Inventory allocation should consider:

- Available stock
- Reserved stock
- Customer order priority
- Product condition
- Location
- Delivery requirements
- Business rules

Inventory should not be allocated if it is quarantined, rejected, or otherwise unavailable for sale.

---

# 17. Picking

Picking controls should verify:

- Order number
- Product
- SKU/product identifier
- Size
- Color
- Quantity
- Condition

Picking errors should be recorded and analyzed.

---

# 18. Packing

Packing should follow approved requirements.

Packing controls may include:

- Correct product
- Correct quantity
- Correct size/color
- Appropriate packaging
- Labels
- Shipping documentation
- Customer/order identification

Packaging requirements should protect the product throughout transportation.

---

# 19. Dispatch

Before dispatch:

- Order accuracy should be confirmed.
- Shipping information should be verified.
- Required documentation should be complete.
- Carrier assignment should be confirmed.
- Tracking information should be recorded where available.

Dispatch timing should be measured against the applicable service commitment.

---

# 20. Logistics Partner & Carrier Management

Carriers and logistics partners should be evaluated based on relevant criteria such as:

- Delivery performance
- Coverage
- Reliability
- Cost
- Tracking capability
- Claims handling
- Responsiveness
- Service quality
- Capacity
- Business continuity

Carrier performance should be reviewed periodically.

---

# 21. Shipment Tracking

Where tracking is available, shipments should be monitored through relevant stages:

**Dispatched → In Transit → Out for Delivery → Delivered**

Exceptions should be identified and communicated to the responsible department.

---

# 22. Delivery Exceptions

Delivery exceptions may include:

- Delayed shipment
- Failed delivery
- Incorrect address
- Damaged shipment
- Lost shipment
- Carrier issue
- Customer unavailable
- Customs or regulatory delay where applicable

Material exceptions should be documented and escalated.

---

# 23. Logistics Cost Management

Logistics costs should be monitored where data is available.

Potential cost components include:

- Freight
- Packaging
- Warehousing
- Handling
- Returns
- Reverse logistics
- Customs/duties where applicable
- Carrier surcharges

Cost increases should be investigated where they materially affect product economics or operating performance.

---

# 24. Returns & Reverse Logistics

Returns should follow a controlled process:

**Return Request → Authorization → Shipment/Collection → Receipt → Inspection → Disposition → Inventory Update → Refund/Replacement Process → Closure**

Return reasons should be captured for analysis.

---

# 25. Returned Product Inspection

Returned products should be evaluated for:

- Product identity
- Condition
- Damage
- Defect
- Signs of use
- Packaging condition
- Resale eligibility

Disposition may include:

- Restock
- Repair/rework
- Quality review
- Replacement
- Disposal
- Other approved disposition

---

# 26. Inventory Reconciliation

Inventory records should be reconciled against physical or operational evidence periodically.

Reconciliation should identify:

- Quantity differences
- Damaged stock
- Missing stock
- Unrecorded movements
- Returns not processed
- Dispatch discrepancies
- Other inventory variances

Material variances should be investigated and documented.

---

# 27. Traceability

Where practical, supply-chain records should provide traceability through:

**Supplier → Purchase/Production → Material/Lot → Warehouse → Inventory → Customer Order → Shipment → Delivery/Return**

Traceability requirements should be proportional to product and operational risk.

---

# 28. Change Control

Changes affecting supply-chain operations should be controlled.

Changes may include:

- Warehouse location
- Carrier
- Logistics partner
- Inventory process
- Packaging
- Fulfillment process
- Delivery service
- Return process
- Inventory system

Material changes should be assessed for cost, quality, delivery, customer, and operational impact.

---

# 29. Supply Chain KPIs

The department shall establish measurement for:

- Inventory accuracy
- Inventory turnover
- Stockout rate
- Inventory aging
- Excess inventory
- Order fulfillment cycle time
- Order accuracy
- On-time dispatch rate
- On-time delivery rate
- Shipping cost per order
- Delivery exception rate
- Return rate
- Return processing time
- Warehouse productivity
- Inventory variance
- Freight cost variance

No KPI values shall be fabricated.

Actual baselines and targets shall be established using verified operational data.

---

# 30. Key Supply Chain Risks

The department shall monitor:

### Inventory Risk
Inventory records or physical stock are inaccurate.

### Stockout Risk
Required products or materials are unavailable.

### Excess Inventory Risk
Inventory exceeds commercially justified requirements.

### Warehouse Risk
Storage, handling, or operational failures damage or misplace inventory.

### Logistics Risk
Carrier or transportation failures affect delivery.

### Cost Risk
Freight, warehousing, or handling costs exceed assumptions.

### Delivery Risk
Customer orders are delayed or incorrectly delivered.

### Returns Risk
Returns are not processed accurately or efficiently.

### Traceability Risk
Supply-chain movements cannot be adequately verified.

### Business Continuity Risk
Disruptions prevent normal supply-chain operations.

---

# 31. Risk Mitigation

Risk should be reduced through:

- Accurate inventory systems
- Cycle counting
- Inventory reconciliation
- Supplier and carrier performance monitoring
- Multiple logistics options where justified
- Shipment tracking
- Exception management
- Inventory planning
- Warehouse controls
- Documented fulfillment procedures
- Return controls
- Business continuity planning

---

# 32. Evidence Standard

Supply-chain records should distinguish between:

- Planned
- Ordered
- In Transit
- Received
- Stored
- Allocated
- Picked
- Packed
- Dispatched
- Delivered
- Returned
- Reconciled
- Closed
- Blocked

Unsupported inventory, delivery, or logistics claims must not be treated as verified.

---

# 33. Definition of Fulfillment Ready

An order is considered fulfillment-ready when:

- Order information is validated.
- Required inventory is available.
- Inventory is not blocked or quarantined.
- Shipping information is available.
- Required packaging is available.
- Required fulfillment instructions are complete.

---

# 34. Definition of Dispatch Ready

An order is dispatch-ready when:

- Correct product and quantity are picked.
- Product condition is acceptable.
- Packing requirements are satisfied.
- Shipping information is verified.
- Required documentation is complete.
- Carrier/transport method is confirmed.

---

# 35. Definition of Supply Chain Closure

A supply-chain transaction may be considered closed when:

- Required movement is completed.
- Delivery or return status is recorded.
- Inventory is updated.
- Relevant financial/logistics information is reconciled.
- Exceptions are resolved or formally escalated.
- Required documentation is complete.

---

# 36. Cross-Department Dependencies

Supply Chain & Logistics coordinates closely with:

**Textile/Fabric Sourcing → Manufacturing → Quality Assurance → Finance → E-commerce → Customer Service → Sales & Marketing → Retail/International Expansion**

Supply-chain decisions must balance inventory efficiency, customer service, delivery reliability, product protection, and financial performance.

# 37. Sustainability & Responsible Logistics

Where commercially and operationally practical, the supply chain should consider:

- Packaging efficiency
- Reduced unnecessary transportation
- Consolidated shipments where appropriate
- Warehouse efficiency
- Reduced material waste
- Reusable/recyclable packaging where appropriate
- Responsible logistics partners
- Reverse-logistics efficiency

Environmental claims must be supported by appropriate evidence.
