# Procurement & Vendor Management Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for Procurement & Vendor Management at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to ensure disciplined sourcing, supplier selection, purchasing, vendor governance, cost control, supplier performance, risk management, ethical procurement, and continuous supplier improvement.

This baseline establishes internal governance and does not constitute legal, tax, or regulatory advice.

## 2. Objectives

Procurement & Vendor Management shall:

- Establish controlled procurement processes.
- Maintain an approved supplier framework.
- Evaluate suppliers objectively.
- Support competitive and responsible sourcing.
- Control purchasing costs.
- Monitor supplier performance.
- Manage supplier risks.
- Protect product quality and delivery requirements.
- Support sustainable procurement.
- Maintain procurement documentation.
- Coordinate supplier contracts with Legal & Compliance.
- Establish measurable procurement KPIs.

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**Procurement Governance → Supplier Discovery → Supplier Qualification → Supplier Selection → RFQ/RFP → Negotiation → Purchase Orders → Contracts → Supplier Performance → Cost Management → Quality → Delivery → Risk → Compliance → Sustainability → Vendor Development → Procurement KPIs**

## 4. Procurement Governance

Procurement shall operate through documented processes covering:

- Supplier selection
- Purchasing
- Approval
- Negotiation
- Contracting
- Purchase orders
- Supplier performance
- Supplier risk
- Supplier exit

Material procurement decisions require appropriate approval.

## 5. Supplier Discovery

Potential suppliers may be identified through:

- Market research
- Industry networks
- Trade events
- Referrals
- Existing supplier networks
- Qualified sourcing platforms
- Direct outreach

Supplier discovery does not constitute approval.

## 6. Supplier Qualification

Suppliers should be evaluated for:

- Capability
- Capacity
- Quality
- Pricing
- Lead time
- Delivery reliability
- Financial stability
- Compliance
- Certifications where relevant
- Sustainability practices
- Communication
- Geographic risk

## 7. Supplier Onboarding

Approved suppliers should provide appropriate:

- Company information
- Contact information
- Banking information
- Tax information where applicable
- Certifications
- Compliance documentation
- Product capability information
- Commercial terms
- Required contractual documents

Supplier records must be maintained securely.

## 8. Supplier Approval

Suppliers should receive documented approval before material purchasing begins.

Approval should consider:

- Commercial suitability
- Quality
- Compliance
- Risk
- Cost
- Capacity
- Strategic fit

## 9. Supplier Segmentation

Suppliers should be segmented according to factors such as:

- Spend
- Strategic importance
- Product criticality
- Supply risk
- Quality risk
- Geographic exposure
- Switching difficulty

Suggested categories include:

- Strategic
- Preferred
- Approved
- Conditional
- Restricted

## 10. Sourcing Strategy

Sourcing decisions should evaluate:

- Total cost
- Quality
- Lead time
- Capacity
- Reliability
- Minimum order quantity
- Payment terms
- Geographic considerations
- Risk
- Sustainability

Lowest unit price should not automatically determine supplier selection.

## 11. RFQ / RFP Process

Competitive sourcing should be used where practical.

Requests should define:

- Product or service specifications
- Quantity
- Quality requirements
- Delivery requirements
- Commercial terms
- Payment terms
- Required certifications
- Submission deadline

Supplier comparisons should use consistent evaluation criteria.

## 12. Supplier Evaluation

Supplier evaluation should consider:

- Price
- Quality
- Delivery
- Capacity
- Responsiveness
- Compliance
- Sustainability
- Financial stability
- Innovation
- Risk

Evaluation criteria should be documented.

## 13. Negotiation

Commercial negotiations may address:

- Unit price
- Volume
- MOQ
- Payment terms
- Lead time
- Quality requirements
- Delivery terms
- Returns
- Warranty
- Tooling
- Packaging
- Freight
- Currency

Material commercial commitments require appropriate approval.

## 14. Total Cost of Ownership

Procurement should evaluate total cost where relevant, including:

- Purchase price
- Freight
- Duties
- Packaging
- Quality costs
- Rework
- Returns
- Storage
- Payment costs
- Supplier switching costs

## 15. Purchase Orders

Purchase orders should contain appropriate:

- Supplier
- Product/service
- Quantity
- Price
- Delivery date
- Delivery location
- Payment terms
- Specifications
- Approval information

Material purchases should follow approved purchasing procedures.

## 16. Contract Coordination

Material supplier contracts should be coordinated with Legal & Compliance.

Contracts should address applicable:

- Scope
- Pricing
- Quality
- Delivery
- Payment
- Intellectual property
- Confidentiality
- Compliance
- Liability
- Termination
- Dispute resolution

## 17. Supplier Performance

Supplier performance should be monitored using:

- On-time delivery
- Quality
- Defect rate
- Fill rate
- Lead time
- Responsiveness
- Cost performance
- Compliance
- Sustainability

## 18. Supplier Scorecards

Strategic and important suppliers should receive periodic scorecards.

Scorecards should identify:

- KPI performance
- Issues
- Corrective actions
- Improvement opportunities
- Overall supplier status

## 19. Supplier Corrective Action

Material supplier failures should follow:

**Detection → Root Cause → Corrective Action → Verification → Closure**

Repeated failures may require escalation or supplier replacement.

## 20. Quality Coordination

Procurement shall coordinate with Quality Assurance and Product Development regarding:

- Specifications
- Samples
- Testing
- Defects
- Inspection
- Corrective actions
- Quality standards

Procurement shall not override quality requirements for price alone.

## 21. Delivery Coordination

Procurement shall coordinate with Supply Chain and Manufacturing regarding:

- Lead times
- Production schedules
- Purchase orders
- Delivery dates
- Capacity
- Shipment requirements

## 22. Cost Management

Procurement should monitor:

- Unit cost
- Cost changes
- Supplier increases
- Currency effects
- Freight
- Duties
- Volume discounts
- Total landed cost

Material cost increases should be escalated.

## 23. Cost Negotiation

Cost negotiations should protect:

- Target margins
- Product quality
- Supplier sustainability
- Delivery performance
- Long-term supplier viability

Unsustainable pricing pressure should be avoided.

## 24. Supplier Risk Management

Key supplier risks include:

- Capacity failure
- Quality failure
- Delivery delays
- Financial instability
- Geographic concentration
- Regulatory risk
- Compliance failure
- Sustainability risk
- Single-source dependency

## 25. Supply Continuity

Critical products and materials should have appropriate continuity plans where justified.

Mitigation may include:

- Dual sourcing
- Alternative suppliers
- Safety stock
- Geographic diversification
- Supplier development
- Contingency logistics

## 26. Single-Source Risk

Single-source suppliers should be identified and assessed based on:

- Product criticality
- Switching difficulty
- Lead time
- Supplier dependency
- Business impact

High-risk single-source relationships should have mitigation plans.

## 27. Supplier Compliance

Suppliers should comply with applicable company requirements concerning:

- Product quality
- Labor practices
- Safety
- Environmental requirements
- Ethical conduct
- Confidentiality
- Intellectual property
- Applicable laws and regulations

## 28. Ethical Procurement

Procurement personnel and suppliers should avoid:

- Bribery
- Kickbacks
- Conflicts of interest
- Undisclosed commissions
- Fraud
- Manipulation of bids
- Improper supplier favoritism

Suspected misconduct should be escalated.

## 29. Conflict of Interest

Employees involved in supplier decisions should disclose material conflicts of interest.

Supplier selection should remain objective and documented.

## 30. Sustainable Procurement

Where commercially and operationally practical, procurement should evaluate:

- Material sustainability
- Supplier environmental practices
- Waste reduction
- Energy efficiency
- Water management
- Packaging
- Responsible sourcing
- Social practices

Sustainability claims must be evidence-based.

## 31. Supplier Diversity

Where practical, Procurement may evaluate a diverse supplier base to improve:

- Resilience
- Innovation
- Competition
- Local sourcing
- Business continuity

## 32. Vendor Data Management

Supplier records should maintain:

- Supplier identity
- Contacts
- Agreements
- Pricing
- Payment terms
- Certifications
- Performance history
- Risk status
- Approval status

## 33. Supplier Financial Information

Where material supplier risk exists, Procurement and Finance may assess:

- Financial stability
- Payment exposure
- Credit risk
- Dependency
- Continuity risk

Sensitive financial information must be access-controlled.

## 34. Procurement Fraud Controls

Controls should address:

- Fake suppliers
- Duplicate suppliers
- Inflated pricing
- Unauthorized purchases
- Invoice fraud
- Kickbacks
- Conflicts of interest
- Unauthorized bank-detail changes

## 35. Segregation of Duties

Where practical, separate:

- Supplier creation
- Supplier approval
- Purchase request
- Purchase approval
- Goods receipt
- Invoice approval
- Payment execution

## 36. Procurement Approvals

The company should maintain documented approval thresholds for:

- Purchase requests
- Purchase orders
- Supplier onboarding
- Contracts
- Price changes
- Emergency purchases

## 37. Emergency Procurement

Emergency procurement may be used when necessary to protect:

- Production continuity
- Customer commitments
- Product safety
- Critical operations

Emergency purchases should be documented and reviewed afterward.

## 38. Procurement Documentation

Procurement records should include, where applicable:

- RFQs/RFPs
- Supplier evaluations
- Quotes
- Negotiation records
- Purchase orders
- Contracts
- Approvals
- Supplier scorecards
- Corrective actions

## 39. Procurement Systems

Procurement systems should support:

- Supplier records
- Purchase orders
- Approvals
- Pricing
- Contract records
- Supplier performance
- Audit trails

## 40. Data Security

Supplier and procurement information should be:

- Access-controlled
- Protected from unauthorized modification
- Appropriately retained
- Backed up
- Restricted to authorized users

## 41. Cross-Department Coordination

Procurement coordinates with:

**CEO Headquarters → Finance & Accounting → Product Development → Textile & Fabric Sourcing → Manufacturing → Quality Assurance → Supply Chain → Legal & Compliance → Sustainability → IT → Sales → Marketing**

## 42. Procurement KPIs

The department shall establish measurement for:

- Procurement savings
- Purchase price variance
- Supplier on-time delivery
- Supplier defect rate
- Supplier lead time
- Supplier response time
- Supplier compliance rate
- Supplier corrective-action closure
- Approved supplier coverage
- Single-source exposure
- Procurement cycle time
- Purchase-order accuracy
- Contract coverage
- Cost avoidance
- Supplier concentration

No KPI values shall be fabricated.

Actual baselines and targets shall be established using verified operational data.

## 43. Key Risks

The baseline addresses:

- Supplier concentration
- Cost inflation
- Quality failures
- Delivery delays
- Supplier financial failure
- Compliance failures
- Fraud
- Ethical risks
- Geographic disruption
- Capacity constraints
- Sustainability risks

## 44. Risk Mitigation

Mitigation may include:

- Supplier diversification
- Supplier qualification
- Performance monitoring
- Contracts
- Quality controls
- Contingency suppliers
- Supplier development
- Risk assessments
- Regular scorecards

## 45. Executive Escalation

Immediate executive escalation should be considered for:

- Critical supplier failure
- Major supply interruption
- Material quality failure
- Significant unexpected cost increase
- Suspected procurement fraud
- Major compliance breach
- Critical single-source exposure

## 46. Continuous Improvement

Procurement should periodically review:

- Supplier performance
- Procurement costs
- Sourcing strategy
- Supplier concentration
- Process efficiency
- Contract performance
- Risk exposure
- Sustainability performance

## 47. Future Capability

Future revisions may introduce:

- Automated supplier scorecards
- AI-assisted sourcing analysis
- Supplier risk monitoring
- Spend analytics
- Automated purchase approvals
- Predictive supplier-risk models
- Digital contract management
- Procurement dashboards

All automation remains subject to appropriate security, privacy, financial, legal, and executive governance.
