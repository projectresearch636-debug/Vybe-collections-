# E-commerce & Website Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for E-commerce & Website at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to provide a reliable, secure, customer-focused, commercially effective digital storefront that accurately represents the brand, products, pricing, inventory, and customer experience.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

- Establish consistent e-commerce operating standards.
- Maintain accurate product information.
- Provide reliable inventory visibility.
- Create a clear and frictionless customer journey.
- Support secure checkout and payment processing.
- Maintain accurate order information.
- Protect customer and business data.
- Establish website performance and availability controls.
- Measure conversion and commercial performance.
- Support scalable digital growth.
- Coordinate website operations with product, inventory, finance, marketing, and customer service teams.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**Product Catalog → Merchandising → Website UX → Inventory Integration → Product Discovery → Cart → Checkout → Payment → Order Management → Customer Account → Customer Communication → Analytics → Security → Optimization**

It applies to Vybe Collections' owned e-commerce website and related digital commerce functionality where applicable.

# 5. Product Catalog Management

Each product should have accurate and approved information before publication.

Product information may include:

- Product name
- SKU/product identifier
- Category
- Description
- Product images
- Size options
- Color options
- Material information
- Care instructions
- Fit information
- Price
- Promotional price where applicable
- Inventory status
- Shipping information
- Return information
- Relevant compliance information

Product information must be consistent with approved product-development records.

---

# 6. Product Publication Readiness

A product should not be published until required information is available.

Publication readiness may include:

- Approved product
- Product identifier
- Approved product name
- Product description
- Product images
- Pricing
- Inventory status
- Size/color information
- Shipping information
- Return information
- Required legal/compliance information
- Required approvals

Incomplete product information should be treated as a publication risk.

---

# 7. Product Merchandising

Merchandising should support:

- Brand positioning
- Customer discovery
- Product relevance
- Commercial objectives
- Inventory availability
- Seasonal priorities
- Product launches
- Promotional strategy

Merchandising decisions should not intentionally create misleading availability, pricing, or product claims.

# 8. Product Discovery

The website should provide clear methods for customers to discover products.

Potential controls include:

- Categories
- Collections
- Search
- Filters
- Sorting
- Related products
- Recommended products
- Featured products
- New arrivals
- Promotional collections

Search and navigation should be monitored for customer friction and product discoverability.

# 9. Product Detail Pages

Product pages should provide sufficient information for informed purchase decisions.

Where applicable, product pages should include:

- Product name
- Images
- Price
- Available sizes
- Available colors
- Product description
- Material composition
- Fit information
- Care instructions
- Availability
- Shipping information
- Returns information

Information must be accurate and consistent with approved company records.

---

# 10. Pricing Controls

Website pricing must align with approved commercial pricing.

Pricing controls should address:

- Standard price
- Promotional price
- Discount
- Currency
- Applicable taxes
- Promotional validity
- Price display
- Pricing changes

Unauthorized pricing changes should not be permitted.

---

# 11. Inventory Integration

Website inventory status should be connected to reliable inventory information where technically feasible.

Inventory states may include:

- In stock
- Low stock
- Out of stock
- Reserved
- Pre-order
- Coming soon
- Unavailable

Inventory information should be synchronized sufficiently to minimize overselling and customer disappointment.

---

# 12. Shopping Cart

The cart should accurately display:

- Product
- Variant
- Quantity
- Price
- Applicable discount
- Estimated totals
- Availability
- Relevant shipping information

Customers should be able to modify or remove items before checkout.

---

# 13. Checkout

The checkout process should be:

- Clear
- Secure
- Mobile-friendly
- Easy to understand
- Transparent about costs
- Designed to minimize unnecessary friction

Checkout should collect only information reasonably required to complete and support the transaction.

---

# 14. Payment Processing

Payment processing must use appropriate secure payment infrastructure.

Controls should address:

- Payment security
- Transaction status
- Failed payments
- Successful payments
- Refunds
- Payment reconciliation
- Fraud-risk controls where applicable

Sensitive payment information should not be unnecessarily stored or exposed.

---

# 15. Order Management

The controlled order lifecycle should be:

**Order Received → Payment Confirmed → Order Validated → Inventory Allocated → Fulfillment → Dispatch → Delivery → Closure**

Order exceptions should be recorded and escalated.

Order status must be communicated accurately to customers.

# 31. E-commerce Risks

The department shall monitor:

### Conversion Risk
Website friction reduces purchases.

### Availability Risk
Website or checkout becomes unavailable.

### Inventory Risk
Website inventory differs from actual available stock.

### Payment Risk
Payment failures prevent successful orders.

### Security Risk
Unauthorized access compromises systems or customer information.

### Data Risk
Analytics or order data is inaccurate.

### Pricing Risk
Incorrect pricing or promotions create financial exposure.

### Customer Experience Risk
Poor website experience damages trust and conversion.

### Integration Risk
Third-party system failures interrupt operations.

### Compliance Risk
Website operations fail applicable legal or regulatory requirements.

# 33. Evidence Standard

E-commerce records should distinguish between:

- Draft
- Approved
- Published
- Active
- Inactive
- Out of Stock
- Order Received
- Payment Confirmed
- Fulfillment Started
- Dispatched
- Delivered
- Returned
- Refunded
- Cancelled
- Closed

Unsupported website, customer, inventory, sales, or conversion claims must not be treated as verified.

# 34. Definition of Product Publication Ready

A product is publication-ready when:

- Product information is approved.
- Pricing is approved.
- Images are available.
- Inventory status is known.
- Variants are configured.
- Shipping information is available.
- Return information is available.
- Required compliance information is addressed.
- Required approvals are documented.

---

# 35. Definition of E-commerce Ready

The e-commerce operation is considered ready when:

- Website is operational.
- Product catalog is configured.
- Inventory integration is functional.
- Checkout is functional.
- Payment processing is functional.
- Order management is operational.
- Shipping information is configured.
- Returns information is available.
- Analytics are functioning.
- Security controls are implemented.
- Required policies are published.
