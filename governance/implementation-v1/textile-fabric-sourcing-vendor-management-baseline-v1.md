# Textile, Fabric Sourcing & Vendor Management Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for Textile, Fabric Sourcing & Vendor Management at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to ensure that fabrics, textiles, trims, components, and suppliers are selected and managed based on quality, commercial viability, availability, reliability, compliance, and brand requirements.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

- Source materials that meet approved product requirements.
- Establish consistent material evaluation standards.
- Identify and qualify reliable suppliers.
- Maintain supplier and material documentation.
- Control material cost and commercial risk.
- Improve supplier reliability and delivery performance.
- Support product quality and manufacturing feasibility.
- Consider sustainability and responsible sourcing where practical.
- Establish measurable sourcing and vendor-management KPIs.
- Reduce supply disruption and supplier concentration risks.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**Material Requirement → Supplier Research → Supplier Qualification → Material Evaluation → Sampling → Testing → Cost Review → Supplier Approval → Purchase → Delivery → Quality Verification → Supplier Performance Review**

It applies to fabrics, yarns, trims, components, packaging materials, and other production inputs where applicable.

## 5. Material Requirements

Each material requirement should define, where applicable:

- Material type
- Fiber composition
- Weight/GSM
- Width
- Construction
- Finish
- Color
- Performance requirements
- Shrinkage requirements
- Colorfastness requirements
- Testing requirements
- Quantity requirement
- Target cost
- Required delivery date
- Sustainability requirements
- Applicable compliance requirements

Material requirements should be documented before final supplier selection.

---

## 6. Supplier Discovery

Potential suppliers may be identified through:

- Industry networks
- Trade shows
- Manufacturer directories
- Verified referrals
- Existing supplier relationships
- Market research
- Approved sourcing channels

Supplier discovery does not constitute supplier approval.

---

## 7. Supplier Qualification

Before a supplier becomes an approved production supplier, appropriate due diligence should be performed.

Evaluation may include:

- Legal business identity
- Manufacturing capability
- Product specialization
- Production capacity
- Quality systems
- Relevant certifications
- Compliance documentation
- Lead times
- Minimum order quantities
- Pricing
- Payment terms
- Geographic/logistics considerations
- Historical performance where available
- Sustainability practices where relevant
- Business continuity risks

The level of due diligence should be proportional to supplier importance and risk.

---

## 8. Material Evaluation

Materials should be evaluated against approved product requirements.

Evaluation may include:

- Appearance
- Hand feel
- Weight/GSM
- Width
- Construction
- Color
- Shrinkage
- Colorfastness
- Durability
- Strength
- Performance characteristics
- Defect level
- Compatibility with manufacturing processes

Material approval should be documented before production use.

---

## 9. Sampling Process

The sourcing process should follow:

1. Material requirement issued.
2. Potential suppliers identified.
3. Supplier quotations requested.
4. Material samples obtained.
5. Samples evaluated.
6. Testing performed where required.
7. Cost and availability reviewed.
8. Supplier/material approved or rejected.
9. Approved material recorded.
10. Production procurement authorized.

Rejected materials should not be used for production unless formally re-approved.

---

## 10. Supplier Commercial Evaluation

Supplier quotations should be evaluated based on total commercial impact, including where applicable:

- Unit material cost
- Minimum order quantity
- Lead time
- Payment terms
- Freight
- Duties/taxes
- Sampling costs
- Testing costs
- Development costs
- Production capacity
- Quality performance
- Delivery reliability

Lowest quoted price should not automatically determine supplier selection.

---

## 11. Supplier Approval Categories

Suppliers may be classified as:

### Approved

Supplier meets required commercial, quality, operational, and compliance requirements.

### Conditional

Supplier may be used subject to defined limitations, corrective actions, or additional approval.

### Development

Supplier is being evaluated or developed and is not yet fully approved for regular production.

### Suspended

Supplier use is temporarily restricted due to unresolved quality, delivery, compliance, financial, or other material risks.

### Rejected

Supplier does not meet company requirements.

---

## 12. Vendor Scorecard

Approved suppliers should be evaluated using measurable criteria such as:

- Quality performance
- On-time delivery
- Price competitiveness
- Lead-time reliability
- Responsiveness
- Minimum order flexibility
- Documentation quality
- Compliance performance
- Corrective-action performance
- Capacity reliability

Supplier performance should be reviewed periodically based on available operational data.

---

## 13. Procurement Controls

Material purchases should have:

- Approved supplier
- Approved material specification
- Agreed quantity
- Agreed price
- Required delivery date
- Appropriate purchase authorization
- Relevant quality requirements

Purchases outside approved sourcing controls require appropriate escalation and authorization.

---

## 14. Quality Control

Material quality should be verified against approved requirements.

Potential controls include:

- Incoming material inspection
- Sample comparison
- GSM/weight verification
- Width verification
- Color verification
- Shrinkage testing
- Colorfastness testing
- Performance testing
- Defect inspection

Non-conforming material should be documented and handled through an appropriate corrective-action process.

---

## 15. Material Traceability

Where practical, materials should be traceable through:

**Supplier → Material → Purchase Order → Lot/Batch → Production → Finished Product**

Material records should be retained according to company documentation requirements.

---

## 16. Supplier Change Control

Changes to an approved supplier or material should be controlled.

Changes may include:

- Supplier
- Manufacturing location
- Fabric composition
- Construction
- Weight/GSM
- Finish
- Color
- Material specification
- Production process

Material or supplier changes that could affect product quality, cost, compliance, or manufacturing must be reviewed before implementation.

---

## 17. Sustainability & Responsible Sourcing

Where commercially and operationally practical, sourcing decisions should consider:

- Responsible material selection
- Recycled or preferred materials where appropriate
- Resource efficiency
- Chemical management
- Supplier environmental practices
- Worker and labor considerations
- Supply-chain transparency
- Relevant certifications

Sustainability claims must be supported by appropriate evidence and must not be represented as verified without documentation.

---

## 18. Key Risks

The department shall monitor:

### Quality Risk
Material fails approved requirements.

### Supply Risk
Supplier cannot provide required quantity or material.

### Price Risk
Material prices increase unexpectedly.

### Lead-Time Risk
Material delays affect product development or production.

### Supplier Concentration Risk
Excessive dependence on a single supplier.

### Compliance Risk
Supplier or material fails applicable requirements.

### Capacity Risk
Supplier cannot support planned production volume.

### Business Continuity Risk
Supplier disruption affects production continuity.

### Counterfeit/Substitution Risk
Unapproved material or supplier substitution occurs.

---

## 19. Risk Mitigation

Risk should be reduced through:

- Supplier qualification
- Approved supplier lists
- Material testing
- Multiple sourcing where justified
- Supplier scorecards
- Contractual controls
- Safety/lead-time planning
- Material specifications
- Approved samples
- Supplier performance monitoring
- Corrective-action procedures

---

## 20. Core KPIs

The department shall establish measurement for:

- Supplier on-time delivery rate
- Material defect rate
- Supplier rejection rate
- Supplier lead-time variance
- Material cost variance
- Supplier price variance
- Purchase order accuracy
- Supplier response time
- Supplier corrective-action closure rate
- Approved supplier coverage
- Supplier concentration
- Material availability rate

No KPI values shall be fabricated.

Actual baselines and targets shall be established from verified operational data.

---

## 21. Evidence Standard

Sourcing and vendor-management records should distinguish between:

- Identified
- Contacted
- Quoted
- Sampled
- Tested
- Qualified
- Approved
- Conditional
- Suspended
- Rejected
- Ordered
- Delivered
- Inspected
- Accepted
- Rejected

Unsupported supplier or material claims must not be treated as verified.

---

## 22. Definition of Approved Supplier

A supplier may be considered approved when:

- Required supplier information is available.
- Relevant due diligence is completed.
- Material capability is confirmed.
- Required samples/testing are satisfactory.
- Commercial terms are reviewed.
- Quality requirements are understood.
- Relevant compliance requirements are addressed.
- Required approvals are documented.

---

## 23. Definition of Approved Material

A material may be considered approved when:

- Material specification is documented.
- Required samples are evaluated.
- Quality requirements are satisfied.
- Required testing is completed.
- Cost and availability are acceptable.
- Product-development requirements are satisfied.
- Required approvals are documented.

---

## 24. Cross-Department Dependencies

The sourcing function coordinates closely with:

**Market Research → Brand Strategy → Fashion Design → Product Development → Finance → Quality Assurance → Manufacturing → Supply Chain → E-commerce → Sales & Marketing**

Sourcing decisions must balance product quality, commercial viability, supply reliability, and long-term company strategy.
