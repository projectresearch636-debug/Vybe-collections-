# Data Analytics & Business Intelligence Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for Data Analytics & Business Intelligence at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to establish a reliable, consistent, secure, and decision-oriented data environment that enables management and departments to measure performance, identify trends, evaluate opportunities, forecast outcomes, and make evidence-based business decisions.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

- Establish company-wide data governance.
- Define standardized business metrics and KPIs.
- Establish reliable data collection processes.
- Improve data quality and consistency.
- Provide accurate management reporting.
- Develop executive and departmental dashboards.
- Support business intelligence.
- Conduct descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive analysis where appropriate.
- Support demand and financial forecasting.
- Provide decision-support insights.
- Protect business and customer data.
- Establish data ownership and accountability.
- Measure analytics effectiveness.
- Enable scalable, data-driven decision making.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**Data Governance → KPI Architecture → Data Collection → Data Quality → Reporting → Dashboards → Business Intelligence → Analytics → Forecasting → Decision Support → Data Security → Analytics KPIs**

# 5. Data Governance

Data governance shall establish:

- Data ownership
- Data stewardship
- Data definitions
- Data classification
- Access requirements
- Data quality standards
- Retention requirements
- Data lineage where appropriate
- Data usage rules

Every critical business dataset should have an accountable owner.

---

# 6. Data Ownership

Critical datasets should identify:

- Dataset
- Business owner
- Technical owner
- Department
- Source system
- Criticality
- Data classification
- Update frequency
- Quality requirements
- Authorized users

Ownership should be reviewed when systems or organizational responsibilities change.

---

# 7. Data Classification

Data may be classified as:

### Public

Information approved for public distribution.

### Internal

Information intended for authorized company use.

### Confidential

Information requiring restricted access.

### Highly Sensitive

Information requiring enhanced controls due to privacy, financial, security, legal, or strategic impact.

Data access should be proportional to classification.

---

Data sources may include:

# 9. Data Collection

Data collection should be:

- Purpose-driven
- Consistent
- Accurate
- Authorized
- Documented
- Secure
- Relevant to the business requirement

Duplicate or unnecessary data collection should be minimized.

---

# 10. Data Integration

Where multiple systems provide information for the same business metric, the company should establish controlled integration or reconciliation processes.

Examples include:

- Sales ↔ Finance
- E-commerce ↔ Inventory
- Marketing ↔ Sales
- Manufacturing ↔ Quality
- Procurement ↔ Finance
- Supply Chain ↔ Inventory
- HR ↔ Finance

Conflicting data should be investigated rather than silently combined.

# 11. Data Quality

Critical data should be evaluated for:

- Accuracy
- Completeness
- Consistency
- Timeliness
- Validity
- Uniqueness
- Integrity

Data-quality issues should be documented and assigned for remediation.

---

# 12. Data Quality Classification

Data-quality issues may be classified as:

### Low

Limited impact and manageable through routine correction.

### Moderate

Impacts departmental reporting or operations.

### High

Materially affects business decisions or important reporting.

### Critical

Could materially affect financial, legal, customer, operational, or executive decisions.

Critical data-quality issues should be escalated promptly.

---

# 13. KPI Architecture

The company shall maintain a standardized KPI architecture.

Each KPI should define:

- KPI name
- Business purpose
- Definition
- Formula
- Unit
- Data source
- Owner
- Reporting frequency
- Target where approved
- Thresholds where applicable
- Calculation logic
- Last update
- Validation status

---

# 14. KPI Hierarchy

KPIs should generally operate at multiple levels:

### Company Level

Measures overall company performance.

### Department Level

Measures departmental contribution.

### Process Level

Measures operational efficiency.

### Product/Channel Level

Measures product, category, channel, or market performance.

KPIs should connect operational performance to strategic objectives.

---

# 15. KPI Governance

A KPI should not be considered an official management metric unless:

- Its definition is documented.
- Its source is identified.
- Its calculation is established.
- Its owner is assigned.
- Its data is validated.

Different departments should not use conflicting definitions for the same company KPI without explicit governance approval.

# 44. Reporting Errors

2. Assessed
3. Corrected

5. Documented

# 56. Data Analytics vs. Market Research

The functions should remain coordinated but distinct.

### Data Analytics & Business Intelligence

Focuses primarily on:

**Internal business data → measurement → analysis → reporting → forecasting → decision support**

**External market/customer information → research → competitive intelligence → trends → market opportunities**

# 64. Analytics Risk Management

Key risks include:

### Data Quality Risk
Incorrect or incomplete data produces incorrect conclusions.

### Definition Risk
Different departments use inconsistent KPI definitions.

### Access Risk
Unauthorized users access sensitive information.

### Privacy Risk
Personal information is improperly used.

### Reporting Risk
Material reports contain errors.

### Forecast Risk
Forecasts are treated as certainty.

### AI Risk
AI-generated insights are accepted without validation.

### Dependency Risk
Critical reporting depends on a single system or person.

---

- Data ownership

- Reconciliation

- Validation

# 66. Executive Escalation

Immediate executive escalation should be considered when:

- A critical KPI is materially incorrect.
- Financial reporting is materially affected.
- Customer or employee data is exposed.
- A critical dashboard fails during an important decision.
- Major data loss occurs.
- Material analytical errors influence decisions.
- A critical data-quality issue remains unresolved.
- Strategic reporting becomes unreliable.
