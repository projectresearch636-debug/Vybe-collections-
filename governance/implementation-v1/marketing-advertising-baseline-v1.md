# Marketing & Advertising Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for Marketing & Advertising at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to build measurable customer demand, strengthen brand awareness, acquire qualified customers, support revenue growth, and protect long-term brand equity through disciplined, evidence-based marketing.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

- Translate company strategy into measurable marketing objectives.
- Build awareness and demand for Vybe Collections.
- Communicate products accurately and compellingly.
- Acquire qualified customers efficiently.
- Support conversion and revenue growth.
- Strengthen customer retention and repeat purchasing.
- Maintain consistent brand communication.
- Measure marketing effectiveness and return on investment.
- Control advertising and promotional risks.
- Coordinate marketing activity across departments.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**Marketing Strategy → Campaign Planning → Content → Organic Marketing → Paid Advertising → Customer Acquisition → Conversion → Retention → Analytics → ROI → Brand Safety**

It applies to marketing and advertising activity conducted through company-owned, paid, earned, partner, and relevant third-party channels.

# 5. Marketing Strategy

Marketing strategy should align with:

- Company strategy
- Brand positioning
- Target customers
- Product strategy
- Commercial objectives
- Available inventory
- Financial requirements

Marketing objectives should be documented and measurable.

Examples may include:

- Brand awareness
- Customer acquisition
- Revenue growth
- Product launch
- Customer retention
- Market expansion

Marketing activity should not operate independently of product, inventory, pricing, or financial realities.

---

# 6. Target Customer

Marketing should define and document relevant target audiences.

Audience definitions may consider:

- Customer demographics
- Customer needs
- Purchase behavior
- Product preferences
- Geographic market
- Customer lifecycle
- Engagement level
- Commercial value

Audience assumptions should be validated through market and customer data where possible.

---

# 7. Campaign Planning

Campaigns should have documented:

- Objective
- Target audience
- Product/service
- Key message
- Creative requirements
- Marketing channels
- Budget
- Start date
- End date
- Responsible owner
- Expected outcome
- Measurement method

Campaigns should be reviewed before launch according to applicable approval requirements.

---

# 8. Marketing Calendar

The department should maintain a coordinated marketing calendar.

The calendar may include:

- Product launches
- Campaigns
- Promotions
- Seasonal events
- Content releases
- Email campaigns
- Social campaigns
- Paid advertising
- Partnerships
- Retail activities

Marketing calendars should be coordinated with product availability and supply-chain readiness.

---

# 9. Content Strategy

Content should support:

- Brand awareness
- Product education
- Product discovery
- Customer engagement
- Community building
- Conversion
- Retention

Content may include:

- Product photography
- Video
- Editorial content
- Social media
- Email
- Educational content
- Product stories
- Campaign creative
- User-generated content where appropriately authorized

Content should remain consistent with approved brand standards.

---

# 10. Creative Governance

Marketing creative should be evaluated for:

- Brand consistency
- Product accuracy
- Visual quality
- Message clarity
- Audience relevance
- Legal/compliance requirements
- Sustainability-claim accuracy where applicable

Creative assets should use approved product information.

---

# 11. Organic Marketing

Organic marketing may include:

- Social media
- Search engine optimization
- Email marketing
- Content marketing
- Community engagement
- Partnerships
- Public relations
- Organic product discovery

Organic activities should have defined objectives and appropriate measurement.

---

# 12. Paid Advertising

Paid advertising should be managed through controlled campaigns.

Potential channels include:

- Search advertising
- Social advertising
- Display advertising
- Video advertising
- Marketplace advertising
- Retargeting
- Other approved digital or offline channels

Channel selection should be based on audience relevance, economics, performance, and strategic fit.

---

# 13. Advertising Budget Governance

Paid marketing budgets should be approved before material expenditure.

Budget controls should identify:

- Approved budget
- Campaign
- Channel
- Planned spend
- Actual spend
- Campaign period
- Responsible owner
- Performance result

Material budget changes require appropriate approval.

---

# 14. Customer Acquisition

Customer acquisition should be measured through the marketing funnel:

**Awareness → Engagement → Consideration → Conversion → First Purchase → Repeat Purchase**

Acquisition channels should be evaluated based on:

- Customer quality
- Acquisition cost
- Conversion
- Revenue
- Margin contribution
- Retention
- Long-term customer value

---

# 15. Marketing Funnel

The marketing funnel should monitor, where measurable:

- Reach
- Impressions
- Traffic
- Engagement
- Product views
- Leads where applicable
- Add-to-cart
- Checkout
- Purchases
- Repeat purchases

Funnel losses should be analyzed to identify optimization opportunities.

---

# 16. Campaign Testing

Where practical, campaigns should use structured testing.

Testing may include:

- Creative
- Headline
- Audience
- Offer
- Landing page
- Channel
- Placement
- Call to action

Tests should have defined variables and measurable outcomes.

---

# 17. Marketing Attribution

Marketing performance should use documented attribution methods.

Attribution should distinguish, where possible:

- Direct
- Organic
- Paid
- Referral
- Email/CRM
- Social
- Partner
- Other measurable sources

Attribution methodology should be consistent when comparing campaigns.

---

# 18. Marketing ROI

Marketing investment should be evaluated against measurable commercial outcomes.

Where sufficient data exists, analysis may include:

- Marketing-attributed revenue
- Marketing spend
- Gross-margin contribution
- Customer acquisition cost
- Return on ad spend
- Customer lifetime value
- Incremental revenue

Revenue attribution should not automatically be treated as incremental revenue.

---

# 19. Promotions

Marketing promotions should be coordinated with:

- Approved pricing
- Inventory
- Product availability
- Margin requirements
- Campaign objectives
- Customer eligibility
- Promotion period

Promotional claims must accurately represent the offer.

- Discounts

Marketing activity should avoid content or placements that create unreasonable:

- Reputational risk
- Legal risk
- Consumer-protection risk
- Platform-policy risk
- Brand-positioning risk

Third-party advertising environments should be monitored where commercially practical.

- Data minimization
- Access control
- Data security
- Retention
- Third-party processing
- Customer preferences
- Opt-out mechanisms where applicable

Customer information must not be used outside authorized purposes.

- Conversion
- Revenue

- Retention

# 28. Core Marketing KPIs

The department shall establish measurement for:

- Marketing-attributed revenue
- Marketing ROI
- Return on ad spend (ROAS)
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- Customer acquisition volume
- Conversion rate
- Website traffic
- Engagement rate
- Cost per click where applicable
- Cost per acquisition
- Email performance
- Repeat purchase rate
- Customer retention rate
- Customer lifetime value where measurable
- Campaign profitability

No KPI values shall be fabricated.

Actual baselines and targets shall be established from verified operating data.

---

# 29. Campaign Performance Review

Campaign reviews should assess:

- Objective achievement
- Spend
- Revenue
- Conversion
- CAC
- ROAS
- Margin contribution
- Audience performance
- Creative performance
- Channel performance
- Customer quality

Poor-performing campaigns should be optimized, paused, or discontinued according to evidence and approved authority.

---

# 30. Marketing Risks

The department shall monitor:

### Brand Risk
Marketing activity damages brand positioning or reputation.

### Financial Risk
Marketing spend fails to produce commercially acceptable outcomes.

### Acquisition Risk
Customer acquisition costs become unsustainable.

### Compliance Risk
Advertising or promotional activity violates applicable requirements.

### Data Risk
Customer or marketing data is inaccurate, misused, or exposed.

### Platform Risk
Dependence on external advertising platforms affects customer acquisition.

### Creative Risk
Marketing content contains inaccurate or unsupported claims.

### Inventory Risk
Marketing demand exceeds available product supply.

### Reputation Risk
Campaigns or partners create negative public response.

---

# 31. Risk Mitigation

Risk should be reduced through:

- Campaign approval
- Budget controls
- Performance monitoring
- Creative review
- Legal/compliance review where required
- Inventory coordination
- Data governance
- Partner screening
- Brand-safety controls
- Campaign testing
- Post-campaign review

---

# 32. Marketing Launch Readiness

A major campaign should be considered launch-ready when:

- Objective is approved.
- Target audience is defined.
- Product/service is ready.
- Inventory availability is confirmed where applicable.
- Pricing/promotion is approved.
- Creative is approved.
- Marketing channels are selected.
- Budget is approved.
- Tracking is configured.
- Legal/compliance requirements are addressed.
- Responsible owners are assigned.

---

# 33. Evidence Standard

Marketing records should distinguish between:

- Planned
- Draft
- Approved
- Scheduled
- Live
- Paused
- Completed
- Measured
- Optimized
- Cancelled

Marketing performance claims must be supported by appropriate data.

Projected performance must not be represented as actual performance.

---

# 34. Cross-Department Dependencies

Marketing & Advertising coordinates closely with:

**Market Research → Brand Strategy → Fashion Design → Product Development → Textile/Fabric Sourcing → Manufacturing → Quality Assurance → Finance → Supply Chain → E-commerce → Sales → Customer Service → Data Analytics**

Marketing decisions must account for product availability, margins, operational capacity, customer experience, and company strategy.

---

# 35. Sustainability & Responsible Marketing

Where commercially practical, marketing should support responsible communication through:

- Accurate sustainability claims
- Evidence-based product stories
- Avoidance of greenwashing
- Responsible content production
- Transparent customer communication

Sustainability claims must be supported by appropriate evidence.

---
