# Fashion Design & Product Development Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for the **Fashion Design & Product Development Department** of Vybe Collections.

The objective is to transform validated market and customer requirements into commercially viable, technically feasible, quality-controlled, and production-ready apparel products.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

* Develop products aligned with brand strategy and target customers.
* Translate customer and market insights into product concepts.
* Maintain design and technical consistency.
* Control product development cost and timelines.
* Establish formal product approval gates.
* Reduce sampling, fit, quality, and production risks.
* Maintain complete and auditable product-development documentation.
* Support commercially viable gross margins.
* Enable a repeatable and scalable product-development process.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers the product-development lifecycle:

**Market & Customer Requirement → Product Concept → Design → Material Selection → Technical Specification → Prototype/Sampling → Fit Review → Cost Review → Quality Review → Production Readiness → Final Product Approval**

It applies to new products, significant product revisions, and new collection development.

## 5. Product Development Gates

| Gate | Stage                       | Required Outcome                       | Primary Owner              |
| ---- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| G0   | Market/Customer Requirement | Validated product opportunity          | Market Research            |
| G1   | Product Concept             | Approved product concept               | Fashion Design             |
| G2   | Design Specification        | Complete design specification          | Design/Product Development |
| G3   | Material Selection          | Suitable material confirmed            | Sourcing                   |
| G4   | Prototype/Sample            | Development sample completed           | Product Development        |
| G5   | Fit & Quality               | Fit and quality requirements satisfied | QA/Design                  |
| G6   | Cost & Margin               | Cost and target margin validated       | Finance                    |
| G7   | Production Readiness        | Manufacturing feasibility confirmed    | Manufacturing              |
| G8   | Final Approval              | Product approved for production        | Authorized Executive       |

A product should not advance to the next controlled gate until the required previous gate has been completed.

---

## 6. Required Product Documentation

Each product should have an identifiable product-development record containing, where applicable:

* Product ID
* Product name
* Collection/season
* Product category
* Target customer
* Design brief
* Product concept
* Technical specification
* Fabric/material specification
* Colorways
* Size range
* Measurement/specification chart
* Bill of Materials (BOM)
* Sample history
* Fit comments
* Quality requirements
* Target product cost
* Target selling price
* Gross-margin target
* Approval status
* Production handoff information

Documentation requirements may be expanded according to product complexity.

---

## 7. Sampling & Fit Process

The sampling process should follow a controlled sequence:

1. Development brief approved.
2. Technical information prepared.
3. Initial sample created.
4. Sample reviewed.
5. Fit and measurement issues documented.
6. Corrections communicated to the relevant supplier/manufacturer.
7. Revised sample produced where required.
8. Fit and quality re-evaluated.
9. Approved sample documented as the production reference.

All significant sample changes should be documented.

---

## 8. Cost & Commercial Review

Before production approval:

* Product cost must be established.
* Material costs must be validated.
* Manufacturing costs must be considered.
* Target selling price must be defined.
* Target gross margin must be assessed.
* Material commercial risks must be identified.

A product that fails the approved commercial requirements must not proceed to production without appropriate authorization.

---

## 9. Quality Requirements

Product development must incorporate quality requirements before production handoff.

Quality review should consider:

* Measurements
* Fit
* Construction
* Stitching
* Fabric/material characteristics
* Color consistency
* Finishing
* Trims and components
* Labeling requirements
* Packaging requirements where applicable

Quality requirements should be documented sufficiently for manufacturing and inspection teams to execute consistently.

---

## 10. Production Handoff

A product may proceed to production only when the required documentation and approvals are complete.

Production handoff should include:

* Approved design
* Approved technical specifications
* Approved material information
* Approved sample/reference
* BOM
* Size specifications
* Quality requirements
* Approved cost
* Production requirements
* Required manufacturing instructions

Incomplete documentation should be treated as a production risk and escalated appropriately.

---

## 11. Change Control

Changes after product approval must be controlled.

Any material change to:

* Design
* Fabric/material
* Measurements
* Construction
* Components
* Cost
* Production method
* Quality requirements

should be documented and assessed for impact.

Where required, affected departments must review and approve the change before implementation.

---

## 12. Core KPIs

The department shall establish measurement for:

* Product development cycle time
* On-time development rate
* First-sample approval rate
* Sample rejection rate
* Average samples per approved product
* Fit approval rate
* Development cost per product
* Target-cost achievement
* Gross-margin achievement
* Production handoff accuracy
* Post-production product defect rate

**No KPI value shall be fabricated.**

Actual targets and baseline values shall be established once sufficient operational data becomes available.

---

## 13. Key Risks

The department shall continuously monitor:

### Demand Risk

Product does not address validated customer demand.

### Design Risk

Product does not maintain brand or design standards.

### Material Risk

Required fabric/material becomes unavailable or commercially unsuitable.

### Fit Risk

Product fails fit or measurement requirements.

### Cost Risk

Actual product cost exceeds the approved commercial model.

### Timeline Risk

Development delays affect collection or production schedules.

### Manufacturing Risk

Product design cannot be efficiently manufactured.

### Quality Risk

Product fails established quality requirements.

### Change-Control Risk

Unapproved changes create cost, quality, or production problems.

---

## 14. Risk Mitigation

Risk shall be reduced through:

* Validated product requirements
* Formal approval gates
* Early material feasibility checks
* Controlled sampling
* Documented fit reviews
* Cost review before production
* QA validation
* Manufacturing feasibility review
* Controlled change management
* Cross-functional approval

---

## 15. Definition of Production-Ready

A product is considered **Production Ready** only when:

* Design is approved.
* Technical specifications are complete.
* Materials are confirmed.
* Required samples are approved.
* Fit requirements are satisfied.
* QA requirements are defined.
* Product costing is approved.
* Target margin is validated.
* Manufacturing feasibility is confirmed.
* Required approvals are documented.

---

## 16. Governance & Decision Rights

The Fashion Design & Product Development Department operates under CEO Headquarters.

The department may make decisions within its approved operational authority.

Decisions involving significant:

* Financial commitments
* Strategic direction
* Brand changes
* Major supplier implications
* Production risks
* Legal/compliance risks
* Exceptions to approved company standards

must be escalated to the appropriate department and/or CEO Headquarters.

---

## 17. Evidence Standard

All product-development reporting must distinguish clearly between:

* **Planned**
* **In Development**
* **Submitted for Review**
* **Approved**
* **Rejected**
* **Production Ready**
* **In Production**
* **Completed**
* **Blocked**

Claims of approval or completion must be supported by documented evidence.

---

## 18. Cross-Department Dependencies

Product development depends on coordinated inputs from:

**Market Research → Brand Strategy → Fashion Design → Sourcing → Finance → QA → Manufacturing → Supply Chain → E-commerce → Sales & Marketing**

No department should optimize its own process at the expense of the overall product lifecycle.
