# Manufacturing, Production & Quality Assurance Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for Manufacturing, Production & Quality Assurance at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to convert approved products and materials into finished apparel through a controlled, efficient, consistent, quality-focused, and commercially viable production process.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

- Establish repeatable production processes.
- Ensure production is based on approved product specifications.
- Verify material and production readiness before manufacturing.
- Manage production capacity and schedules.
- Maintain consistent product quality.
- Detect and control defects early.
- Reduce rework, rejection, and production losses.
- Establish formal quality inspection and release controls.
- Monitor manufacturing and quality performance.
- Maintain traceable production records.
- Support on-time and commercially disciplined production.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**Production Planning → Factory/Vendor Qualification → Product & Material Readiness → Production Scheduling → Cutting/Manufacturing → In-Process Quality → Finishing → Final Inspection → Defect Management → Corrective Action → Production Release → Performance Review**

It applies to internal and outsourced manufacturing operations where applicable.

# 5. Production Readiness

Production should not begin until required readiness conditions have been satisfied.

Production readiness should include, where applicable:

- Approved product design
- Approved technical specifications
- Approved material
- Approved sample/reference
- Confirmed BOM
- Confirmed size specifications
- Defined quality requirements
- Manufacturing feasibility confirmation
- Production quantity
- Production schedule
- Required purchase/work orders
- Required approvals

Incomplete production information must be treated as a production risk.

---

# 6. Factory & Manufacturing Vendor Qualification

Manufacturing partners should be evaluated according to their importance and risk.

Evaluation may include:

- Legal business identity
- Manufacturing capabilities
- Product specialization
- Production capacity
- Equipment
- Workforce capability
- Quality systems
- Lead times
- Minimum order requirements
- Cost competitiveness
- Compliance requirements
- Previous performance
- Corrective-action capability
- Business continuity
- Sustainability practices where relevant

Qualification does not automatically guarantee ongoing approval.

Performance must be monitored after production begins.

# 7. Production Planning

Production planning should consider:

- Approved order quantity
- Available materials
- Manufacturing capacity
- Production lead time
- Required delivery date
- Workforce availability
- Equipment requirements
- Quality requirements
- Dependencies
- Production risks

Production plans should be documented and updated when material changes occur.

---

# 8. Production Scheduling

Production schedules should define, where applicable:

- Product
- Quantity
- Manufacturing partner
- Planned start date
- Planned completion date
- Required delivery date
- Production status
- Dependencies
- Responsible owner

Production schedule changes should be documented where they materially affect delivery or commercial commitments.

# 9. Production Execution

Manufacturing should follow approved product and production specifications.

Production controls may include:

- Cutting
- Sewing/assembly
- Printing
- Embroidery
- Washing/finishing
- Trimming
- Pressing
- Labeling
- Packaging

Any significant deviation from approved requirements must be identified and reviewed.

---

# 10. In-Process Quality Control

Quality should be monitored during production rather than relying solely on final inspection.

In-process checks may include:

- Measurements
- Construction
- Stitching
- Seam quality
- Fabric defects
- Color consistency
- Print/embroidery quality
- Component placement
- Finishing
- Workmanship

Significant recurring defects should trigger investigation and corrective action.

---

# 11. Quality Inspection

Quality inspections should be performed at appropriate production stages.

Inspection may include:

### Incoming Material Inspection

Verify materials against approved specifications.

### First-Article / Initial Production Review

Confirm that production output matches the approved product reference.

### In-Process Inspection

Identify defects before large quantities are completed.

### Final Inspection

Verify finished products against approved quality requirements before release.

Inspection methodology should be appropriate to product volume, risk, and customer requirements.

---

# 12. Defect Classification

Defects should be classified consistently.

## Critical Defect

A defect that may create significant safety, legal, regulatory, or serious customer risk.

Critical defects require immediate escalation.

## Major Defect

A defect that materially affects product function, appearance, fit, durability, or customer acceptance.

Major defects require corrective action before product release where applicable.

## Minor Defect

A defect that has limited impact on product appearance or function but does not meet the defined quality standard.

Minor defects should be monitored and controlled according to approved acceptance criteria.

Actual acceptance thresholds must be established according to product requirements and applicable standards.

---

# 13. Non-Conformance Management

When a product or material does not meet requirements:

1. Identify the non-conformance.
2. Document the issue.
3. Classify the defect.
4. Determine affected quantity.
5. Identify the root cause where appropriate.
6. Define corrective action.
7. Implement corrective action.
8. Verify effectiveness.
9. Record the outcome.
10. Release, rework, reject, or otherwise disposition the affected goods according to approved authority.

No material or product should be released outside approved requirements without documented authorization.

---

# 14. Corrective & Preventive Action

Recurring or significant issues should be handled through a structured corrective-action process.

Corrective-action records should identify:

- Problem
- Date
- Product
- Supplier/manufacturer
- Affected quantity
- Root cause
- Corrective action
- Responsible owner
- Target completion date
- Verification result
- Closure status

Preventive actions should be implemented where recurring risks are identified.

---

# 15. Rework & Rejection

Rework should be controlled and documented.

Records should identify:

- Reason for rework
- Quantity affected
- Rework owner
- Rework cost where measurable
- Reinspection result

Rejected goods should be segregated and handled according to approved disposition procedures.

---

# 16. Production Release

Finished products may be released for downstream distribution only after required quality and production controls have been completed.

Production release should confirm, where applicable:

- Quantity verification
- Final quality inspection
- Defect status
- Rework completion
- Packaging requirements
- Labeling requirements
- Required documentation
- Release authorization

---

# 17. Production Traceability

Where practical, production should be traceable through:

**Product → Manufacturer → Work Order → Material/Lot → Production Batch → Inspection → Finished Goods → Shipment**

Production records should be retained according to company documentation requirements.

---

# 18. Change Control

Changes after production approval must be controlled.

Changes may include:

- Product specification
- Material
- Construction
- Manufacturing method
- Supplier/manufacturer
- Production quantity
- Quality requirements
- Packaging
- Labeling

Changes that may affect quality, cost, delivery, compliance, or customer experience must be reviewed before implementation.

# 21. Key Manufacturing Risks

The department shall monitor:

### Capacity Risk
Manufacturing partner cannot support required volume.

### Schedule Risk
Production cannot meet required delivery dates.

### Quality Risk
Finished goods fail approved standards.

### Material Risk
Required materials are unavailable or unsuitable.

### Cost Risk
Actual manufacturing costs exceed approved assumptions.

### Supplier Risk
Manufacturing partner performance deteriorates.

### Workforce Risk
Labor availability affects production.

### Equipment Risk
Equipment failure affects production continuity.

### Compliance Risk
Manufacturing operations fail applicable requirements.

### Business Continuity Risk
Unexpected disruption affects production.

---

# 22. Risk Mitigation

Risk should be reduced through:

- Manufacturing partner qualification
- Production capacity reviews
- Production planning
- Approved specifications
- Material readiness checks
- In-process inspection
- Final inspection
- Supplier scorecards
- Corrective-action processes
- Production contingency planning
- Appropriate supplier diversification
- Documented escalation procedures

---

# 23. Quality Release Principle

Quality assurance has authority to identify and escalate products that do not meet approved requirements.

Production pressure must not override documented quality requirements without appropriate executive authorization.

---

# 24. Evidence Standard

Production and QA records should distinguish between:

- Planned
- Scheduled
- In Production
- Inspected
- Passed
- Failed
- Reworked
- Rejected
- Released
- Shipped
- Blocked
- Closed

Unsupported production or quality claims must not be treated as verified.

---

# 25. Definition of Production Ready

A production order is considered ready when:

- Product specifications are approved.
- Required materials are available.
- Manufacturing capability is confirmed.
- Quality requirements are defined.
- Production quantity is confirmed.
- Production schedule is established.
- Required documentation is complete.
- Required approvals are documented.

---

# 26. Definition of Quality Approved

A product is considered quality approved when:

- Required inspections are completed.
- Defined quality requirements are satisfied.
- Critical/major issues are resolved or formally dispositioned.
- Required rework is completed.
- Final inspection requirements are satisfied.
- Release authorization is documented.
