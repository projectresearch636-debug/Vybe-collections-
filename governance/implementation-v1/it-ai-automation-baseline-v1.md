# IT, AI & Automation Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for IT, AI & Automation at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to provide secure, reliable, scalable, and appropriately governed technology capabilities that support business operations while protecting company systems, data, employees, customers, intellectual property, and business continuity.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

- Establish IT governance and technology ownership.
- Maintain an accurate technology and system inventory.
- Protect company systems against cybersecurity threats.
- Apply appropriate identity and access controls.
- Establish data governance and protection practices.
- Govern responsible use of artificial intelligence.
- Develop reliable business automation.
- Maintain software and technology lifecycle controls.
- Establish backup and recovery capabilities.
- Maintain technology incident-response procedures.
- Assess technology vendor risks.
- Support business continuity and disaster recovery.
- Measure technology performance using verified KPIs.
- Enable scalable digital operations.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**IT Governance → Infrastructure → Cybersecurity → Access Control → Data Governance → AI Governance → Automation → Software Systems → Backup & Recovery → Incident Response → Vendor Technology Risk → Technology KPIs**

It applies to company-owned and company-managed:

- Hardware
- Software
- Cloud services
- Websites
- Applications
- Databases
- Networks
- Business systems
- AI systems
- Automation workflows
- Technology vendors
- Digital infrastructure

- Dependencies

- Hardware
- Software

- Cloud services
- Websites
- Domains
- Databases

- AI systems

- Technology vendors

# 8. System Classification

Systems may be classified according to business criticality:

### Critical

Failure could materially disrupt company operations, security, revenue, customers, or regulatory obligations.

### High

Failure could significantly affect a department or important business process.

### Moderate

Failure creates manageable operational disruption.

### Low

Failure has limited business impact.

System criticality should influence security, backup, monitoring, recovery, and access requirements.

---

Technology infrastructure should be designed for:

- Reliability

- Availability
- Maintainability

- Appropriate cost efficiency

Infrastructure may include:

- Cloud services
- Servers
- Networks
- End-user devices

- Communication systems

---

# 10. Cybersecurity Governance

The company should maintain reasonable security controls appropriate to its size, systems, data, and risk profile.

Security controls should address:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Access management
- Device security
- Network security
- Software security
- Data protection
- Monitoring
- Vulnerability management
- Incident response
- Security awareness

---

# 11. Identity & Access Management

Access should follow the principle of least privilege.

Access requests should identify:

- User
- System
- Required access
- Business justification
- Approver
- Effective date
- Review requirement

Access should be removed or modified when employment or responsibilities change.

---

# 12. Authentication

Where technically and operationally appropriate, systems should use strong authentication controls.

Controls may include:

- Strong passwords
- Multi-factor authentication
- Single sign-on
- Session management
- Device authentication
- Privileged-access controls

Authentication requirements should be proportional to system risk.

---

# 13. Privileged Access

Administrative and privileged access should be:

- Limited
- Authorized
- Monitored where appropriate
- Reviewed periodically
- Removed when no longer required

Shared privileged accounts should be avoided where practical.

---

# 14. Joiner, Mover & Leaver Controls

Technology access should follow employee lifecycle events.

### Joiner

New access is granted based on role and approval.

### Mover

Existing access is reviewed when responsibilities change.

### Leaver

Access is removed promptly following employment termination or other applicable offboarding events.

Human Resources and IT should coordinate these processes.

---

# 15. Access Reviews

Critical-system access should be reviewed periodically.

Reviews should identify:

- Inactive users
- Excessive permissions
- Former employees
- Unnecessary access
- Privileged accounts
- Unapproved accounts

Remediation should be documented.

---

# 16. Data Governance

Data should have appropriate:

- Ownership
- Classification
- Access controls
- Retention
- Backup
- Integrity controls
- Security controls

Data governance should coordinate with Legal & Compliance, Finance, HR, Customer Service, E-commerce, and other relevant departments.

---

# 17. Data Classification

Information may be classified as:

### Public

Information intended for public distribution.

### Internal

Information intended for authorized company use.

### Confidential

Information requiring restricted access.

### Highly Sensitive

Information requiring enhanced controls due to potential legal, financial, security, privacy, or reputational impact.

Classification should guide access and handling requirements.

---

# 18. Customer Data

Customer information should be:

- Collected appropriately
- Used only for authorized purposes
- Accessed only by authorized personnel
- Protected against unauthorized disclosure
- Retained only as appropriate
- Deleted or anonymized according to applicable requirements

---

# 19. Employee Data

Employee information should receive appropriate privacy and access controls.

Access should be limited to authorized personnel with a legitimate business need.

---

# 20. Data Integrity

Critical business data should be protected against:

- Unauthorized modification
- Accidental deletion
- Corruption
- Inconsistent records
- Unauthorized extraction

Systems should maintain appropriate validation and reconciliation controls where required.

---

# 21. Software Governance

Software should be:

- Approved where required
- Appropriately licensed
- Secure
- Maintained
- Documented
- Monitored for business relevance

Unauthorized software should not be installed on company systems where prohibited by company policy.

---

# 22. Software Lifecycle

Software lifecycle management should cover:

**Requirement → Evaluation → Approval → Implementation → Testing → Deployment → Monitoring → Maintenance → Retirement**

Critical changes should follow appropriate change-management procedures.

---

# 23. Change Management

Material technology changes should identify:

- Change owner
- Business purpose
- Systems affected
- Risk
- Testing
- Rollback approach
- Approval
- Deployment timing
- Monitoring

Emergency changes should be documented after implementation where advance approval is not practical.

---

# 24. Testing

Material technology changes should be tested before production deployment where practical.

Testing may include:

- Functional testing
- Security testing
- Integration testing
- Performance testing
- Data validation
- User acceptance testing
- Recovery testing

---

# 25. Website & E-commerce Technology

# 26. API & Integration Governance

APIs and system integrations should be:

- Documented

- Monitored where appropriate
- Tested

Integration failures should have appropriate error handling and escalation.

# 27. AI Governance

AI systems used by Vybe Collections should be governed according to:

- Business purpose
- Data sensitivity
- System risk
- Accuracy requirements
- Human oversight
- Security
- Privacy
- Legal requirements
- Vendor risk

AI should support business objectives without replacing required human accountability.

---

# 28. Approved AI Use

AI may be considered for:

- Data analysis
- Forecasting
- Customer-service assistance
- Marketing support
- Product-development support
- Administrative automation
- Workflow optimization
- Research
- Software development
- Reporting

Use must remain consistent with company policy and applicable requirements.

---

# 29. Restricted AI Use

AI systems should not independently make material decisions involving:

- Employment
- Compensation
- Legal conclusions
- Customer remedies
- Financial commitments
- Security responses
- Regulatory responses

unless an approved process specifically provides for such use with appropriate human oversight.

---

# 30. Human Oversight

Material AI outputs should be reviewed by an appropriately responsible person before consequential action.

Human review should consider:

- Accuracy
- Relevance
- Bias or unfairness
- Privacy
- Security
- Legal implications
- Business impact

---

# 31. AI Data Protection

Confidential, sensitive, personal, or proprietary information must not be entered into AI systems unless the system and use have been appropriately authorized.

AI vendors should be assessed for relevant:

- Data handling
- Privacy
- Security
- Retention
- Access
- Model-training practices
- Contractual protections

---

# 32. AI Output Validation

AI-generated outputs should be treated as potentially fallible.

Validation should be proportional to the consequences of the output.

High-impact outputs require stronger human review.

---

# 33. AI Transparency

Where appropriate, users and affected stakeholders should be informed when AI is materially involved in a process.

The company should maintain sufficient documentation of important AI systems to support accountability.

---

# 34. AI System Inventory

The company should maintain an inventory of material AI systems including:

- System name
- Business purpose
- Owner
- Vendor
- Data used
- Risk level
- Human oversight
- Key dependencies
- Review status

---

# 35. Automation Governance

Automation should be designed to:

- Improve efficiency
- Reduce repetitive work
- Improve consistency
- Reduce avoidable errors
- Maintain appropriate controls

Automation should not remove necessary accountability.

---

# 36. Automation Lifecycle

The controlled automation process is:

**Business Need → Process Mapping → Risk Assessment → Design → Development → Testing → Approval → Deployment → Monitoring → Review → Retirement**

---

# 37. Automation Controls

Material automated workflows should have:

- Owner
- Purpose
- Trigger
- Inputs
- Processing logic
- Outputs
- Failure handling
- Monitoring
- Human override where appropriate
- Documentation

---

# 38. Automation Failure Handling

Automations should fail safely where practical.

Failure handling should include:

- Error detection
- Logging
- Notification
- Manual fallback
- Recovery
- Root-cause analysis where appropriate

---

# 39. Human Override

Critical automated processes should provide an appropriate mechanism for human intervention.

Automation must not create uncontrolled financial, legal, customer, or operational commitments.

---

# 40. Business Continuity

Technology continuity planning should identify:

- Critical systems
- Critical dependencies
- Recovery priorities
- Backup requirements
- Recovery procedures
- Responsible personnel
- Communication procedures

---

# 41. Backup

Critical data should have appropriate backup arrangements.

Backup controls should consider:

- Frequency
- Retention
- Security
- Integrity
- Recovery requirements
- Access control
- Geographic or logical separation where appropriate

---

# 42. Backup Testing

Backups should be tested periodically where practical.

Testing should confirm that:

- Backups exist
- Backups are usable
- Data can be restored
- Recovery procedures are documented

A successful backup does not automatically prove successful recovery.

---

# 43. Disaster Recovery

Critical systems should have recovery plans appropriate to their business importance.

Recovery planning should consider:

- Recovery time objective
- Recovery point objective
- System dependencies
- Personnel
- Infrastructure
- Data
- Communication
- Validation

---

# 44. Technology Incident Management

Technology incidents should follow:

**Detection → Classification → Containment → Investigation → Recovery → Validation → Closure → Lessons Learned**

Material incidents should be escalated promptly.

---

# 45. Security Incidents

Security incidents may include:

- Unauthorized access
- Malware
- Phishing
- Credential compromise
- Data exposure
- Suspicious activity
- System compromise
- Denial-of-service
- Lost or stolen devices

Potential security incidents should be reported immediately through approved channels.

---

# 46. Incident Severity

Incidents may be classified as:

### Low

Limited impact with routine remediation.

### Moderate

Meaningful operational or security impact requiring coordinated response.

### High

Significant business, security, customer, financial, or regulatory impact.

### Critical

Severe impact requiring immediate executive coordination.

---

# 47. Incident Documentation

Incident records should include where appropriate:

- Date and time
- Detection method
- Affected system
- Severity
- Impact
- Actions taken
- Responsible personnel
- Recovery status
- Root cause where identified
- Corrective actions
- Closure

---

# 48. Vulnerability Management

Technology vulnerabilities should be:

- Identified
- Assessed
- Prioritized
- Remediated
- Verified

# 49. Security Awareness

- Device security
- Data handling

# 50. Technology Vendor Risk

- Reliability
- Data processing
- Business continuity
- Financial stability
- Contractual obligations
- Compliance

# 51. Third-Party Access

Third-party access should be:

- Authorized
- Limited
- Time-bound where appropriate
- Monitored where practical
- Removed when no longer required

Vendor access should not exceed business requirements.

---

# 52. Technology Contracts

Technology contracts should appropriately address, where relevant:

- Services
- Security
- Privacy
- Data processing
- Availability
- Support
- Service levels
- Intellectual property
- Confidentiality
- Incident notification
- Termination
- Data return/deletion

# 53. Vendor Offboarding

When a technology vendor relationship ends:

- Access should be removed
- Data should be returned/deleted where required
- Credentials should be revoked
- Assets should be recovered where applicable
- Contract obligations should be reviewed
- Documentation should be retained

- Inadequate documentation

# 57. Evidence Standard

Technology governance records should distinguish between:

- Proposed
- Approved
- In Development
- Testing
- Active
- Suspended
- Deprecated
- Retired

Unverified system or security claims must not be represented as confirmed facts.

# 58. Technology KPIs

The department shall establish measurement for:

- System availability
- Critical incidents
- Mean time to detect
- Mean time to resolve
- Backup success rate
- Recovery-test success rate
- Security incident count
- Vulnerability remediation rate
- Access-review completion
- Security-training completion
- Automation success rate
- Critical-system recovery readiness
- Technology project delivery
- Technology cost efficiency where measurable

No KPI values shall be fabricated.

Actual baselines and targets shall be established using verified operational data.

---

# 59. Technology Risk Management

IT, AI & Automation should continuously monitor:

### Cybersecurity Risk
Unauthorized access, attacks, vulnerabilities, or data exposure.

### Availability Risk
Critical systems become unavailable.

### Data Risk
Data is lost, corrupted, exposed, or improperly used.

### AI Risk
AI outputs or decisions create inaccurate, unfair, insecure, or non-compliant outcomes.

### Automation Risk
Automated workflows create uncontrolled or incorrect actions.

### Vendor Risk
Technology suppliers fail to meet security, availability, or contractual requirements.

### Continuity Risk
The company cannot recover critical technology after disruption.

### Technology Debt Risk
Legacy systems reduce scalability, security, or efficiency.

---
