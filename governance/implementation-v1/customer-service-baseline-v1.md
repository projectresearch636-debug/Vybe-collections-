# Customer Service Baseline v1

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the controlled operating baseline for Customer Service at Vybe Collections.

The objective is to provide timely, accurate, respectful, and consistent customer support while resolving issues efficiently, protecting customer trust, and creating a structured feedback loop into product, operations, and commercial decision-making.

---

## 2. Objectives

The department shall:

- Provide consistent customer support.
- Respond to customer requests within defined service standards.
- Resolve customer issues efficiently.
- Provide accurate order and product information.
- Manage complaints through controlled escalation.
- Coordinate returns and exchanges.
- Support refunds and replacements.
- Protect customer information.
- Measure customer satisfaction and service performance.
- Identify recurring customer problems.
- Feed customer insights back into relevant departments.
- Protect the reputation and long-term customer relationships of Vybe Collections.

---

## 3. Scope

This baseline covers:

**Customer Support → Order Support → Product Questions → Delivery Issues → Returns/Exchanges → Complaints → Escalation → Resolution → Customer Satisfaction → Retention Feedback → Analytics**

It applies to customer-service activity across applicable digital, retail, social, email, telephone, messaging, and other approved channels.

# 5. Customer Support Channels

Approved customer-service channels may include:

- Email
- Website support
- Contact forms
- Messaging
- Telephone
- Social-media support
- Retail support
- Other approved channels

Each active channel should have a clearly assigned owner.

---

# 6. Customer Inquiry Management

Customer inquiries should be:

1. Received
2. Identified
3. Categorized
4. Assigned
5. Responded to
6. Resolved
7. Documented
8. Closed

Unresolved inquiries should remain visible until resolution or formal escalation.

---

# 7. Customer Identification

Where necessary to resolve an issue, customer identity and order information should be appropriately verified.

Customer information should only be requested when reasonably necessary.

Sensitive customer information must not be unnecessarily exposed through customer-service communications.

---

# 8. Issue Classification

Customer-service issues should be categorized consistently.

Potential categories include:

- Product information
- Product availability
- Order status
- Payment
- Shipping
- Delivery
- Product quality
- Product defect
- Return
- Exchange
- Refund
- Cancellation
- Complaint
- Website issue
- Promotional issue
- Other

Classification should support reporting and root-cause analysis.

---

# 9. Response Standards

Customer-service response standards should define:

- First-response target
- Resolution target
- Escalation target
- Communication expectations
- Business-hours coverage where applicable

Actual service targets should be established based on company capacity, customer expectations, and operating data.

---

# 10. Customer Communication

Customer communication should be:

- Clear
- Accurate
- Respectful
- Professional
- Consistent
- Timely
- Solution-oriented

Customer-service representatives should not make unsupported promises regarding:

- Delivery dates
- Refunds
- Replacements
- Discounts
- Product availability
- Compensation

unless authorized and supported by company information.

---

# 11. Product Questions

Product-related questions should be answered using approved information.

Relevant information may include:

- Product specifications
- Size
- Fit
- Material
- Care instructions
- Color
- Availability
- Shipping
- Returns

Unverified product claims should not be communicated as facts.

---

# 12. Order Support

Customer-service order support may include:

- Order confirmation
- Order status
- Payment status
- Inventory availability
- Fulfillment status
- Dispatch status
- Delivery status
- Cancellation requests
- Order corrections

Customer-facing order information should match the authoritative operational record.

---

# 13. Payment Issues

Payment-related customer issues should be handled through controlled coordination with Finance and applicable payment systems.

Potential issues include:

- Payment failure
- Duplicate payment
- Payment pending
- Refund pending
- Incorrect payment status

Customer-service staff should not independently alter financial records unless authorized.

---

# 14. Shipping & Delivery Support

Delivery issues may include:

- Delayed shipment
- Failed delivery
- Incorrect address
- Damaged shipment
- Lost shipment
- Tracking issue
- Carrier issue

Customer Service should coordinate with Supply Chain & Logistics for operational verification.

Customers should receive accurate status information based on available evidence.

---

# 15. Cancellation Requests

Cancellation requests should follow approved company policy.

Before confirming cancellation, relevant factors may include:

- Order status
- Payment status
- Fulfillment status
- Dispatch status
- Product availability
- Applicable customer policy

Unauthorized cancellation commitments should not be made.

---

# 16. Returns

Returns should follow a controlled process:

**Customer Request → Eligibility Review → Return Authorization → Collection/Shipment → Receipt → Inspection → Disposition → Refund/Replacement → Closure**

Return policies must be clearly communicated to customers.

---

# 17. Exchanges

Where exchanges are offered, the process should establish:

- Eligibility
- Product condition
- Available replacement
- Size/color availability
- Return requirements
- Shipping responsibilities
- Financial adjustments where applicable

Exchange commitments should be based on verified inventory and company policy.

---

# 18. Refunds

Refund requests should be processed according to approved policy and applicable payment procedures.

Customer Service should communicate:

- Refund eligibility
- Refund status
- Expected processing information where available
- Required actions

Refund completion should be verified through the appropriate financial record.

---

# 19. Complaints

Customer complaints should be:

1. Recorded
2. Categorized
3. Assessed
4. Assigned
5. Investigated
6. Resolved or escalated
7. Communicated to the customer
8. Closed
9. Reviewed for recurring causes where appropriate

Complaints should be treated as a source of operational and customer insight.

---

# 20. Complaint Severity

Complaints may be classified according to impact.

Potential severity levels:

### Level 1 — Routine
Minor issue with limited customer impact.

### Level 2 — Significant
Issue requires cross-department involvement or extended resolution.

### Level 3 — Critical
Issue creates material customer, legal, financial, safety, security, or reputation risk.

Critical complaints should be escalated promptly.

---

# 21. Escalation

Escalation may be required when:

- Customer issue cannot be resolved within the defined service standard.
- Product quality is disputed.
- Safety concerns are raised.
- Financial exposure is material.
- Legal/compliance concerns exist.
- Customer information may be compromised.
- Multiple customers report the same issue.
- A significant reputation risk exists.
- Senior management intervention is required.

Escalation ownership should be clearly assigned.

---

# 22. Customer Safety Issues

Any customer report involving potential product safety concerns should be treated as high priority.

The issue should be:

- Recorded
- Escalated
- Investigated
- Coordinated with Quality Assurance and Legal/Compliance where appropriate
- Controlled according to applicable procedures

Customer-service personnel should not make unsupported technical or legal conclusions.

---

# 23. Customer Data Protection

Customer data must be handled according to applicable privacy and data-protection requirements.

Controls should address:

- Access control
- Data minimization
- Secure communication
- Data retention
- Authorized use
- Third-party access
- Customer privacy requests
- Incident escalation

Customer information must not be disclosed to unauthorized parties.

---

# 24. Customer-Service Knowledge Base

The department should maintain controlled reference information covering:

- Product information
- Shipping policies
- Returns
- Exchanges
- Refunds
- Order procedures
- Frequently asked questions
- Escalation procedures
- Approved communication guidance

Knowledge-base information should be reviewed periodically for accuracy.

---

# 25. Customer Communication Templates

Where templates are used, they should support consistency while allowing appropriate personalization.

Templates may cover:

- Order confirmation support
- Shipping updates
- Delivery delays
- Return instructions
- Refund status
- Product information
- Complaint acknowledgment
- Resolution communication

Templates must not contain inaccurate or outdated information.

---

# 26. Customer Satisfaction

Customer satisfaction should be measured using appropriate methods.

Potential measures include:

- CSAT
- Customer effort
- Resolution satisfaction
- Post-interaction feedback
- Complaint outcomes
- Repeat-contact patterns

Customer feedback should be analyzed for trends rather than treated only as individual cases.

---

# 27. First-Contact Resolution

Where practical, Customer Service should aim to resolve customer issues during the initial interaction.

First-contact resolution should not come at the expense of:

- Accuracy
- Policy compliance
- Product quality
- Financial controls
- Customer safety

Complex issues should be escalated rather than incorrectly resolved.

---

# 28. Service Backlog

The department should monitor unresolved customer cases.

Backlog reporting should identify:

- Open cases
- Age of cases
- Priority
- Assigned owner
- Escalated cases
- Cases approaching service limits
- Overdue cases

Material backlog increases should be investigated.

---

# 29. Root-Cause Analysis

Recurring customer issues should be analyzed to identify underlying causes.

Potential root causes may include:

- Product defects
- Product information errors
- Website problems
- Fulfillment errors
- Shipping failures
- Packaging issues
- Marketing communication
- Pricing errors
- Policy confusion

Findings should be shared with the responsible department.

---

# 30. Customer Feedback Loop

Customer feedback should flow into:

**Customer Service → Data/BI → Product → Quality → Supply Chain → E-commerce → Marketing → Brand → Executive Management**

Relevant feedback should inform:

- Product improvements
- Website improvements
- Service improvements
- Marketing communication
- Operational changes
- Product development
- Customer retention strategy

---

# 31. Customer-Service KPIs

The department shall establish measurement for:

- First-response time
- Resolution time
- First-contact resolution rate
- Customer satisfaction (CSAT)
- Customer complaint rate
- Return-related contacts
- Escalation rate
- Repeat-contact rate
- Customer retention
- Support backlog
- Resolution SLA attainment
- Case reopen rate
- Customer effort where measurable

No KPI values shall be fabricated.

Actual baselines and targets shall be established from verified operational data.

---

# 32. Customer-Service Reporting

Periodic reporting should include:

- Case volume
- Case categories
- Response performance
- Resolution performance
- Complaint trends
- Return-related issues
- Customer satisfaction
- Escalations
- Backlog
- Recurring problems
- Recommended corrective actions

Significant trends should be escalated to CEO Headquarters.

---

# 33. Customer-Service Risks

The department shall monitor:

### Service Risk
Customers do not receive timely support.

### Resolution Risk
Issues remain unresolved or are incorrectly resolved.

### Reputation Risk
Poor service damages customer trust or brand reputation.

### Data Risk
Customer information is mishandled or exposed.

### Product Risk
Recurring product issues are not communicated to Quality or Product Development.

### Operational Risk
Customer commitments do not match actual inventory, fulfillment, or delivery capability.

### Financial Risk
Unauthorized refunds, discounts, or compensation create financial exposure.

### Compliance Risk
Customer communication or complaint handling fails applicable requirements.

---

# 34. Risk Mitigation

Risk should be reduced through:

- Service standards
- Case ownership
- Escalation procedures
- Knowledge management
- Customer-data controls
- Accurate operational information
- Quality feedback
- Complaint analysis
- Staff training
- Performance monitoring

---

# 35. Customer-Service Readiness

Customer Service is considered operationally ready when:

- Support channels are defined.
- Ownership is assigned.
- Policies are available.
- Product information is accessible.
- Order information is accessible.
- Return procedures are defined.
- Escalation procedures are established.
- Customer-data controls are implemented.
- Reporting requirements are established.
- Required staff training is completed.

---

# 36. Evidence Standard

Customer-service records should distinguish between:

- Received
- Open
- Assigned
- Pending Customer
- Pending Internal
- Escalated
- Resolved
- Closed
- Reopened

Customer-service claims must be supported by the relevant operational record where applicable.

Projected resolution dates must not be presented as confirmed outcomes unless verified.

---

# 37. Cross-Department Dependencies

Customer Service coordinates closely with:

**E-commerce → Supply Chain & Logistics → Quality Assurance → Product Development → Finance → Marketing → Sales → Legal & Compliance → Data Analytics**

Customer-service decisions must remain aligned with company policies, inventory availability, product quality, financial controls, and legal requirements.

---

# 38. Continuous Improvement

The department should periodically review:

- Customer feedback
- Complaint trends
- Service performance
- Resolution efficiency
- Product issues
- Website issues
- Delivery issues
- Return reasons

Improvement initiatives should be prioritized according to customer impact, frequency, cost, and strategic importance.
